A design that does not know which existing rules bind it will either re-decide
something already settled or contradict it without noticing. This is the claim
that the surrounding rules were found and read — not that they were obeyed, and
not that anyone agreed with them.

The usual mistake is to treat it as a search performed once, at the beginning.
The body of rules a design sits inside keeps moving while the design is being
written: rules are added, narrowed, superseded and retired, and a claim made
against the old picture buys nothing against the new one. Standing next to a
design and asking *what governs this* is work that has to be redone whenever
the answer could have changed.

Discharged thinly, it fails late and expensively. A design built on the wrong
governing picture reads perfectly well on its own — there is nothing in it to
notice. The contradiction surfaces when someone who knows the rule reads the
design, or, more often, after the work has already landed.
