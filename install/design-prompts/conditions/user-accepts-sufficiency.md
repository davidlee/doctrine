Somebody has to say that enough has been asked. Without that judgement,
inquiry either stops at an arbitrary point or does not stop at all, and
neither produces a design worth relying on.

The mistake is to read this as following automatically from the questions
having been dealt with. Dealing with every question that was raised says
nothing about whether the right questions were raised. This is the separate
and harder judgement — that the set itself was adequate to the decision being
made, and that the areas nobody asked about were areas where nothing was at
stake.

Given carelessly, it produces the failure that looks most like success: a
design answering a complete set of the wrong questions, with a clean record
showing every one of them closed. Nothing later in the process is looking for
that, because everything later takes this judgement as given.
