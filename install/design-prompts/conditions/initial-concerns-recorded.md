Before a design commits to an answer, the questions that could invalidate it
belong on the table, and somebody other than whoever wrote them down should
have looked at the list. This is those two halves together, and the order
between them is the substance: a look that happened before the questions were
proposed looked at nothing.

What goes wrong here is nearly always scope. This is not the point at which
the questions get answered — it asks only that the concerns be stated and
seen, and a later obligation is what closes them out. The opposite failure is
padding: a long list assembled to look thorough becomes the list that must be
worked through, and every question added here is one somebody has to dispose
of before the design can move again.

A thin discharge produces a design that proceeds on a framing nobody
challenged. That is the most expensive class of error to find late, because
everything built on the framing has to come apart with it.

**What is enough.** The user reviews the map as `doctrine design tree SL-NNN`
renders it — it marks the blocking questions and states any `needs` edges. Do
not draw your own listing. Ask: "Anything missing, or
wrongly marked blocking? Otherwise, 'reviewed'." Record their review of that
listing in one submission — the tree and its marks are the whole of what they
are confirming, so there is no separate set to declare.
