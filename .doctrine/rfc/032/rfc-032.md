# RFC-032: Review ledger effectiveness

## Context

The RV review ledger (`RV-NNN`, ADR-007) is Doctrine's adversarial-review
substrate: append-only findings, a turn-based baton, derived status, a
severity-gated close tooth. It has been stable since SL-040 built it
(2026-06-11) and has since been wired into `/audit`, `/code-review`,
`/inquisition`, `/reconcile`, and the managed design run.

A research pass over the originating slice, ADR-007, the successor slices
(SL-061/080/109/147/234/260), RFC-004, RFC-026, the open backlog, and ~35
review-specific friction observations finds that the mechanism is sound and its
*edges* are the problem. Two failures are systematic:

- **The read-back surface was never designed.** ADR-007 specifies how findings
  are written and barely how they are read. `review show` renders status and
  brief plus a finding *count*; the tier is reachable only via `--json`. Recorded
  nine times across four slices.
- **The protocol's load-bearing moves have no durable field.** `raise`/`dispose`
  write durable prose; `contest`/`verify` keep only an ephemeral `--note`, so the
  reasoning that forces revisions lands in a gitignored baton or in chat.

Around those sit a design-run integration cluster (the run's own pass RV is
invisible; an externally conducted RV cannot be named), an inherited
identity/reservation footgun shared with every kind, and a governance gap: ADR-007
is the *only* artifact that owns the kind — no PRD/SPEC covers it — and both
ADR-007 (D-C8 empty ledger, D-C10 warm-cache) and the shipped
`install/review-ledger.md` have drifted from the implementation.

**The evidence and full analysis live in [`research.md`](./research.md)** — 13
findings (F1–F13), an analysis (A1–A8), and the candidate list (C1–C27). This RFC
owns the programme and its sequencing; it does not restate the evidence.

## Discussion

### What this is

A programme of several slices, not one change. The RFC's job is to fix the
sequencing and the governance route so the slices land coherently; the candidate
list is already decomposed to actionable entities (table below), so the RFC can
point rather than restate.

### Decisions

The pivotal design decisions (D1–D15), their options, and what each gives up
live in [`decision-frontier.md`](./decision-frontier.md). It went through three
external review passes (codex), and the user settled both of its preference forks
on 2026-09-26. That document is this programme's design proposal. Slice designs
lock it piece by piece; it is not canon until they do or a REV promotes it.

Three structural choices carry most of the programme:

- **`D1` — per-finding turn journal.** Contest/verify reasoning becomes durable.
  `amend`/`reopen` acts are added. The baton's counters become derivable.
- **`D4`/`D5` — an engine-tier ledger module with one read projection.** `show`
  lists findings by default. CLI, MCP, the census and cross-kind status all read
  one view.
- **`D9` — clone-wide id reservation** in its own local ref namespace.

Plus one status rule (`D2`): `done ⇔ all findings terminal ∧ concluded`. It is
uniform, derived on every read, and there is no legacy backfill. `conclude`
takes a required `--basis`.

### Sequencing — ledger first, consumers after

The original order put design-run unification first, because `ISS-314` `needs`
`IMP-392`. That edge is stale: the concluded-pass marker it waited on landed on
2026-08-07. With that premise gone, the dependency order runs from the ledger
to its consumers. The hardest, hardest-to-reverse choices are schema choices,
so "hard parts first" still holds.

| order | unit | decisions / items | why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0a | backlog item | `CHR-057` (verb to retract a `needs` edge), then retract `ISS-314 needs IMP-392` | the stale edge makes slice 1 read as blocked |
| 0b | assessment | `IMP-481` via `/spec-coverage-assessment` | fixes the new tech spec's scope and parent before slice 1 authors it |
| 0c | backlog item | quick-win finding index + `list --target` over existing fields (split from `IMP-475`), built on a view struct | nine recorded incidents; no schema dependency |
| 1 | **Ledger v2** | D4 (split, `IMP-029` golden first), D1, D2, D15, D10, D8 write side; authors the tech spec | every later slice reads this schema |
| 2 | **Read surface** | D5 remainder (projection, census, JSON unification), D14 | needs D4's module and D1's columns |
| 3 | **Design-run binding** | D3 (anchor + its column), D6 (bind-before, `External` disposition arm, `Finding` deletion, change row, `ISS-462`) | a consumer of the ledger |
| 4 | **Identity & locus** | D9, then D7 | D7 is unsafe before D9 |
| — | rides the slice that touches it | D11 (`prime`; else slice 1), D12 (close `IMP-479`), D13's doc-conformance check (slice 1 or 2) | reconciliation, not work of its own |

0a and 0b land before slice 1's design locks. Slices 3 and 4 are independent of
each other and can run in parallel. `IMP-482` (foreign-scoped commits in a phase
boundary) is dispatch-side and independent of this order; it can land whenever.
`ISS-310` / `ISS-359` are attestation questions and stay outside the programme.

### Governance route

Drive design; mint a **REV** once each slice's design locks; promote it during
reconcile.

| slice | governance change |
|---|---|
| 1 | ADR-007 D-C5 (turns, amend/reopen), D-C8 (uniform `done`, the conclude marker's role), and D-C10 if `prime` is touched (`D11`, selector model); new tech spec authored |
| 3 | SPEC-029 — bind step, bind-before admission, `External` arm, change row |
| 4 | ADR-007 D-C1/D-C7 — locus tiers; one writer per RV at a time, git merge a best-effort backstop; PRD-005 / SPEC-008 — `reach = local` means "this clone" |

The tech spec grows in each slice. Pass-running skills (`/audit`, `/code-review`,
`/inquisition`, the design run) and `install/review-ledger.md` (`CHR-079`) are
updated in the slice whose behaviour they describe.

### Open questions

The three original questions are answered by the frontier:

- **Slice 1 boundary** — the design-run cluster (C10–C12) is not slice 1. It lands
  whole in slice 3, except `ISS-310` / `ISS-359`.
- **Does a spec own the RV kind?** — leading proposal: a tech spec for the ledger
  mechanism, authored in slice 1. `IMP-481` (step 0b) fixes its scope and parent.
  Reservation and the design-run binding stay in SPEC-008 and SPEC-029.
- **Close-gate cost** — no reverse index until one is measured to be needed
  (`D12`; close `IMP-479`).

None remain open at programme level. New questions belong to the slice designs.

## Outcome

Open. The programme proposal is settled (`decision-frontier.md`, 2026-09-26);
nothing has landed. This RFC exists to sequence a programme and to anchor it
to actionable entities; it should move to `resolved` when the front-loaded slices
have landed their governance and the remaining workstreams are either done or
explicitly deferred.
