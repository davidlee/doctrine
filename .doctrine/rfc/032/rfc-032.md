# RFC-032: Review ledger effectiveness

## Context

The RV review ledger (`RV-NNN`, ADR-007) is Doctrine's adversarial-review
substrate: append-only findings, a turn-based baton, derived status, a
severity-gated close tooth. It has been stable since SL-040 built it
(2026-06-11) and has since been wired into `/audit`, `/code-review`,
`/inquisition`, `/reconcile`, and the managed design run.

A research pass over the originating slice, ADR-007, the successor slices
(SL-061/080/109/147/234/260), RFC-004, RFC-026, the open backlog, and ~35
review-specific friction observations finds that the mechanism is sound and its
*edges* are the problem. Two failures are systematic:

- **The read-back surface was never designed.** ADR-007 specifies how findings
  are written and barely how they are read. `review show` renders status and
  brief plus a finding *count*; the tier is reachable only via `--json`. Recorded
  nine times across four slices.
- **The protocol's load-bearing moves have no durable field.** `raise`/`dispose`
  write durable prose; `contest`/`verify` keep only an ephemeral `--note`, so the
  reasoning that forces revisions lands in a gitignored baton or in chat.

Around those sit a design-run integration cluster (the run's own pass RV is
invisible; an externally conducted RV cannot be named), an inherited
identity/reservation footgun shared with every kind, and a governance gap: ADR-007
is the *only* artifact that owns the kind — no PRD/SPEC covers it — and both
ADR-007 (D-C8 empty ledger, D-C10 warm-cache) and the shipped
`install/review-ledger.md` have drifted from the implementation.

**The evidence and full analysis live in [`research.md`](./research.md)** — 13
findings (F1–F13), an analysis (A1–A8), and the candidate list (C1–C27). This RFC
owns the programme and its sequencing; it does not restate the evidence.

## Discussion

### What this is

A programme of several slices, not one change. The RFC's job is to fix the
sequencing and the governance route so the slices land coherently; the candidate
list is already decomposed to actionable entities (table below), so the RFC can
point rather than restate.

### Proposed sequencing — hard parts first

> **Superseded proposal (2026-09-26).** The companion
> [`decision-frontier.md`](./decision-frontier.md) re-cuts this order, and it was
> revised after an external design review. The original order below rested on
> `ISS-314 needs IMP-392`, and that edge is stale: its prerequisite, the
> concluded-pass marker, landed on 2026-08-07. The current proposal puts the
> ledger first and its consumers after:
>
> 0. `CHR-057` (retract a `needs` edge) → retract `ISS-314 needs IMP-392`;
>    the `IMP-481` coverage assessment; the quick-win finding index.
> 1. **Ledger v2** — engine-tier ledger module, per-finding turn journal,
>    empty-ledger status plus a `complete` predicate for dependency gates,
>    fail-safe reads, file/stdin args; authors the tech spec.
> 2. **Read surface** — one read projection, census, JSON unification, RV as a
>    relation target.
> 3. **Design-run binding** — section anchors, bind/adopt a pass (SPEC-029
>    revision), delete the runtime `Finding`.
> 4. **Identity & locus** — clone-wide reservation (PRD-005/SPEC-008 revision),
>    then the three-tier locus rule.
>
> Slices 3 and 4 can run in parallel. The original order is kept below for the
> record until a slice design settles the matter.

Original proposal. Per the guiding principle that the expensive decisions should
be settled while the blast radius is smallest:

1. **Slice 1 — design-run / RV unification (WS-B).** Land `DEC-125`/`IMP-392`: one
   finding model, RV findings carrying a document-section reference and its
   fingerprint; retire the runtime `Finding`; resolve the invisible-pass-slot and
   externally-conducted-RV traps (`ISS-476` fixed, `ISS-322` open). This is the
   `needs` of the status-model work, so it goes first. It should **leave behind
   spec coverage** for the unified finding model.
2. **Slice 2 — read surface & status model (WS-A).** Now buildable on one model:
   render the finding tier (`IMP-475`), unify CLI/MCP shapes (`IMP-476`), add the
   census surface (`IMP-477`), and settle the empty-ledger status model (`C1` —
   `empty ∧ ¬concluded ⇒ active/raiser`, `empty ∧ concluded ⇒ done`, using the
   existing `conclude` marker). Leaves spec coverage for the RV read contract.
3. **Slice 3 — protocol durability & safety (WS-C).** Durable contest/verify
   rationale and amend/reopen (`ISS-280`, `ISS-485`), shell-safe/file args
   (`ISS-486`, `IMP-377`), the locus-guard omission (`ISS-484`), fail-safe enum
   reads (`CHR-001`).
4. **Slice 4 — identity, provenance & coverage (WS-D).** Reservation reach and
   reseat (`ISS-279`, `ISS-277`), RV as a relation target (`IMP-480`),
   phase-boundary foreign scope (`IMP-482`), and the governance-coverage
   assessment that decides whether a PRD/SPEC owns the kind (`IMP-481`).

Slices 1–2 are deliberately the two biggest; 3–4 are smaller and may be
re-ordered or split as operational evidence arrives. `C25` (`ISS-059`, the
literal-selector arm of `prime`) is a small fix that can fold into Slice 3.

### Governance route

Drive design; mint a **REV** once the design locks; promote it during reconcile.
Two candidates are governance changes and ride their slices rather than getting
their own:

- **ADR-007 D-C8** (empty ledger) — amended by Slice 2's REV, alongside the
  status-model change.
- **ADR-007 D-C10** (warm-cache) — reconciled by whichever slice touches it
  (`IMP-478`); the ADR text is currently contradicted by RFC-004 + SL-147, so the
  REV either rewrites D-C10 to the lived selector model or re-affirms it and
  opens the reader.

Slices that establish new spec coverage author it in-slice; `IMP-481` is the
assessment that fixes the boundary. C20 (`IMP-479`, close-gate reverse lookup) and
C14 (disposition vocabulary/route) ride whatever slice makes them concrete;
RFC-026's `P10` trial already owns C14's convention.

### Open questions

`decision-frontier.md` §3 proposes answers to all three: the design-run cluster
moves to slice 3; `IMP-481` runs before slice 1 and fixes the tech spec's
boundary; no close-gate index until one is measured to be needed.

- **Slice 1 boundary.** `IMP-392` is currently scoped as "unify design-run
  findings onto the RV ledger" but its `needs` tree has grown (`ISS-314`). How
  much of the design-run cluster (C10–C12) lands in Slice 1 versus a follow-on?
- **Does a spec own the RV kind?** Settled by Slice 4's assessment (`IMP-481`),
  but it conditions how much of Slice 1–2's output is REV versus SPEC.
- **Close-gate cost.** Whether the `D-C9b` scan needs an index (`IMP-479`) depends
  on where review count lands; no cliff measured yet.

## Outcome

Open. Nothing concluded. This RFC exists to sequence a programme and to anchor it
to actionable entities; it should move to `resolved` when the front-loaded slices
have landed their governance and the remaining workstreams are either done or
explicitly deferred.
