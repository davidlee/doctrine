# RFC-026: Design review response effectiveness

This RFC asserts no canon; it is a discussion to drive solution design.

## Context

Complex designs often exhibit degenerate review/response
phase behaviour and results:
- responses fail to apply sufficient rigour to validating their assertions with evidence, so the reviewer finds what the author should have at less expense
- responses introduce new failures with each successive revision; often overcorrecting on the finding's dimension, while neglecting others.
- responses address the instance, not the class: for example, a specific stale governance, without checking for similar cases
- sister records / storage tiers not updated uniformly, leading to avoidable drift which costs a round 

And more generally:
- review may gradually drift design attention from what's actually relevant to "spider fucking" hypothetical scenarios completely irrelevant to the context of use or scope of a slice
- acronym obsession leads to human-incomprehensible coordinate-speak between agents 
- design artefacts after heavy review absorb so much history that the underlying design is no longer apparent
- design becomes anchored in locally evolved and obfuscatory terms of art, rather than use cases, user experience, and real-world impact 
- reaching review acceptance (as a hard gate) can become a more expensive and still less complete route to finding defects than proceeding to implementation
- design activity, when it occurs outside a slice's designated stage, lacks framework support and integration
- there's no obvious mechanism for splitting an unmanageably large slice; no epic, etc
- large slices often leave design "in the small" unspecified for the plan or phase plan to drive out; lack of symmetry with these efforts and their review mechanisms (ad-hoc)
- no skill or framework support for actually integrating feedback effectively to correct these issues
- orchestration of raiser / responder is left as an exercise for the reader.
