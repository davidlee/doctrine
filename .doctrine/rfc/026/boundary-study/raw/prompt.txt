Instrument for RFC-026 E11. Three prompts, run in order on 2026-09-19 by a
read-only research agent; output is ../classification.md (sections 1-4, 5, 6).

=== 1. Base classification ===
Read-only research in the doctrine repo. Do not edit, create, or commit anything.

TOOLS. Use the CLI, not raw files: `doctrine review show RV-NNN`,
`doctrine slice show SL-NNN`, `doctrine review list`. Check `--help` for flags;
do not guess them. Prefer ./target/debug/doctrine if it exists, else `doctrine`.

QUESTION. When design review fails to converge, what are the findings about?
We are testing this hypothesis: most blocker/major design-review findings
concern things that are cheap to reverse and that a thin implementation would
settle. Only a minority concern commitments that are costly to reverse.

POPULATION.
  Group N (non-convergent / heavily reviewed), fixed list:
    RV-325 (reviews SL-233), RV-370 (reviews SL-246), RV-307 (reviews SL-230),
    the design-facet ledger(s) on SL-232, on SL-249, and on SL-238.
  Group C (comparison): the 6 most recent design-facet ledgers that concluded
    with 10 or fewer findings. Select by that rule only; list the ids you
    picked and the command that produced the list.

FOR EACH LEDGER report: slice id, a one-sentence description of what the slice
changes, design.md line count, rounds, and total findings by severity.

FOR EVERY blocker and major finding (all of them, no sampling; if a ledger
makes that infeasible, say so and state exactly which findings you covered),
assign exactly ONE class:

  A  COSTLY SURFACE: concerns a commitment that is expensive to reverse:
     persisted authored format/schema or corpus migration; public CLI or skill
     surface that agents depend on; binding governance (ADR/SPEC/POL/STD/REQ);
     confinement or security boundary; a released artefact.
  B  MECHANISM PREDICTION: the design asserted code-level detail (call chain,
     type, signature, data shape, reachability, file location) that was wrong
     or unestablished, and that compiling or implementing a thin version would
     have exposed.
  C  ARTEFACT PROSE: concerns the document itself: internal inconsistency,
     stale restatement of a fact owned elsewhere, review history/notes
     sections, citations, numbering, wording.
  D  MISSING INTENT: could not be resolved because a human-owned input was
     absent: no stated adversary, threat model, unacceptable outcome, scope
     boundary, or product intent.
  E  OTHER: say what, in under 10 words.

Also mark each finding R if its target text was written to repair an earlier
finding (a repair-of-a-repair), else leave blank.

OUTPUT (markdown, compact):
  1. One table per group: ledger | slice | design lines | rounds | findings
     (blocker/major/minor/nit) | count of A / B / C / D / E | count marked R.
  2. One flat table of the classified findings: ledger | F-N | severity |
     class | R | <=12-word reason. This is the evidence; keep it complete.
  3. Totals: A/B/C/D/E percentages for Group N and for Group C, side by side.
  4. At most 10 lines of caveats: ambiguous classifications (give F-N ids and
     the two classes you hesitated between), ledgers you could not read, and
     anything that contradicts the hypothesis.

RULES. Classify from the finding's own text and its disposition, not from the
slice title. When unsure between two classes, pick one and list it under
caveats. Do not speculate; write "unknown" rather than guess. Do not summarise
the design or recommend anything.

=== 2. Delegation test (added to FOR EACH LEDGER) ===
Also report: does the design delegate its normative statements to knowledge
records (DEC/ASM/EVD etc.) rather than restating them — yes / partial / no,
with one line of evidence.

=== 3. B1/B2 split (class-B rows only) ===
For each class-B finding, split: B1 = a compile or a thin happy-path
implementation would expose it; B2 = only a hostile or edge-case input
would expose it (implementation compiles and passes ordinary tests).
