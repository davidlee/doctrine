QUESTION. What are these design-review findings about, and which of their targets
were written to repair an earlier finding?

POPULATION. Every blocker and major finding on these three design-facet ledgers,
all of them, no sampling — 18 findings in total:
  RV-374 (reviews SL-261, "Design adopt verb")
  RV-382 (reviews SL-262, "Envelope names the next move")
  RV-377 (reviews SL-263, "Ambient memory surfacing for pi and codex")

FOR EACH LEDGER report: slice id, a one-sentence description of what the slice
changes, design.md line count at the design lock, rounds, contests, and total
findings by severity.

FOR EVERY blocker and major finding, assign exactly ONE class:

  A  COSTLY SURFACE: concerns a commitment that is expensive to reverse:
     persisted authored format/schema or corpus migration; public CLI or skill
     surface that agents depend on; binding governance (ADR/SPEC/POL/STD/REQ);
     confinement or security boundary; a released artefact.
  B  MECHANISM PREDICTION: the design asserted code-level detail (call chain,
     type, signature, data shape, reachability, file location) that was wrong
     or unestablished, and that compiling or implementing a thin version would
     have exposed.
  C  ARTEFACT PROSE: concerns the document itself: internal inconsistency,
     stale restatement of a fact owned elsewhere, review history/notes
     sections, citations, numbering, wording.
  D  MISSING INTENT: could not be resolved because a human-owned input was
     absent: no stated adversary, threat model, unacceptable outcome, scope
     boundary, or product intent.
  E  OTHER: say what, in under 10 words.

Then mark each finding with these three flags, each independent:

  R  its target text was written to repair an earlier finding in the same
     ledger (a repair-of-a-repair). Else leave blank.
  L  a LATER finding in the same ledger concerns the same mechanism, or the
     text written to repair this one. Else leave blank.
  B2 (class-B findings only) only a hostile or edge-case input would expose it:
     a thin happy-path implementation compiles and passes ordinary tests.
     Otherwise the finding is B1 (a compile or a thin happy-path implementation
     would expose it).

OUTPUT (markdown, compact):
  1. One table per ledger: ledger | slice | design lines | rounds | contests |
     findings (blocker/major/minor/nit) | count of A / B / C / D / E |
     count marked R | count marked L.
  2. One flat table of the classified findings: ledger | F-N | severity |
     class | R | L | B1/B2 | <=12-word reason. This is the evidence; keep it
     complete.
  3. Totals across the 18: A/B/C/D/E counts and percentages; count of R; count
     of L; of the class-B findings, how many are B1 vs B2.
  4. At most 10 lines of caveats: ambiguous classifications (give F-N ids and
     the two classes you hesitated between), ledgers or findings you could not
     read, and anything that contradicts the question's premise.

RULES. Classify from the finding's own text and its disposition, not from the
slice title. When unsure between two classes, pick one and list it under
caveats. Do not speculate; write "unknown" rather than guess. Do not summarise
the design or recommend anything.
