QUESTION. A routing convention is in use on design-review ledgers, where each
blocker or major finding is assigned one route from a closed set of five. Your
job: assign each severe finding on the ledgers below a route, and say plainly
where none fits.

THE FIVE ROUTES. Assign exactly one, or the literal `none`.

| route | the question behind the finding | what settles it |
|---|---|---|
| `review` | should we accept this commitment and its consequences? | design judgement and adversarial review |
| `demonstrate` | can these parts connect as proposed? | a thin implementation exercising the disputed connection — "it compiles" is not the bar |
| `probe` | does the mechanism withstand the adversary? | a stated adversary, then a hostile probe |
| `control` | would the planned check notice failure? | a negative control: name the concrete incorrect candidate the check must reject, and observe it rejected |
| `owner-fix` | do two accounts of one fact disagree? | remove the duplicate, verify the surviving owner, sweep the affected class |

When more than one route fits, route on the claim whose failure would make the
rest of the finding moot.

`none` means: the finding is real and severe, but none of the five route
questions is the question it is actually asking. Use `none` when it is the
honest answer, and say in <=12 words what question the finding *is* asking. Do
not strain to fit a route, and do not invent a sixth route.

SECOND LABEL. For every row you route `owner-fix` or `none`, add exactly one:
  `duplicate` — two live accounts of one fact exist; settled by deleting one.
  `stale`     — a record lags the thing it describes; settled by editing it.
  `neither`   — both descriptions are wrong for this finding.
Leave it blank on other rows.
