# RFC-027: Progressive discovery and proof-bearing plans

> Status: **open** — deliberative architectural proposal and prove-value guide.
> This RFC proposes principles, hypotheses, candidate design surfaces, and
> falsifiers. It asserts no canon (ADR-014), does not settle a final schema, and
> does not supersede RFC-003, RFC-004, RFC-023, RFC-025, RFC-026, SPEC-002, or the
> orchestration work currently under review.

## Context

Doctrine’s selectors, plans, phase gates, execution controls, and coverage machinery are converging on an end-to-end chain of proof:

```text
requirement
→ design intent
→ phase obligation
→ intended change
→ assertion
→ execution evidence
→ requirement coverage
```

The existing pieces already demonstrate substantial value:

* selectors distinguish scope-relevant, design-target, and actual-delta paths;
* Git supplies immutable evidence of what changed;
* phase gates can constrain completion;
* coverage can relate evidence to requirements;
* reconciliation separates observed reality from authored truth;
* confinement prevents workers from silently extending their own authority.

Several RFC-023 repairs have also shipped. `check plan` now checks VT shape and
whether non-waived VT test files fit design-target selectors, and the VT gate can
distinguish evidence that predates the slice as unattributable. SPEC-002
REQ-254–REQ-257 and the `verify` / `coverage verify` seam already resolve and run
project-declared, language-neutral commands as Git-anchored observed evidence.
The remaining gap is therefore not simply “checks run too late” or “commands are
not executable”; it is the semantic continuity between intent, prediction,
discovery, criterion revision, and that existing evidence substrate.

The current selector model nevertheless carries only path, intent, and an optional note. Its principal derived view is the declared-versus-actual path differential. This is effective at finding divergence, but not at explaining it.

RFC-023 identifies several consequences, some now partially mitigated:

* behavioural gates may currently be satisfied by textual presence;
* required file touches outside VT test paths are still discovered manually;
* undeclared paths can be surfaced to the control plane, but carry little causal
  or semantic explanation and cannot be authored into the plan by a worker;
* objectives may have no owning phase;
* immutable criterion IDs become tombstones when their purpose changes.

These defects plausibly share a pressure, but this RFC does not assume that one
data model will solve all of them:

> The plan is treated as a complete declaration of future execution, while execution is treated primarily as evidence of conformance or violation.

Real implementation is also a process of discovery. Dependencies emerge, assumptions fail, secondary effects become visible, assertions prove inadequate, and designs become more concrete. Doctrine currently represents progressive concreteness weakly and progressive discovery mostly as drift.

The result is expensive in several ways:

1. An overlooked file touch is treated as a governance violation before Doctrine can distinguish discovery from negligence.
2. Path drift carries insufficient semantic metadata to explain why it occurred.
3. Robust plans require repeated adversarial prose review and manual cross-checking.
4. Confined workers can identify a defective contract but have little structured ability to challenge it.
5. A realization expressed redundantly across plans, prose, gates, and selectors must be corrected in every copy.
6. Immutable gate identities preserve history by leaving semantically incorrect criteria active, neutered, or disguised as tombstones.

## Thesis

Planning should advance along two independent axes:

```text
progressive concreteness

requirement
→ objective or obligation
→ phase responsibility
→ intended change
→ assertion
→ executable procedure
→ evidence
```

```text
progressive discovery

predicted
→ questioned
→ discovered
→ classified
→ authorized or rejected
→ reconciled
```

A second trace of the concreteness axis, added **2026-08-08**, runs through
proof rather than through material change:

```text
requirement
→ governing design intent
→ obligation
→ verification criterion
→ proof binding
→ execution evidence
```

Read as: a **requirement** owns normative product or technical truth at its
existing altitude; **governing design intent** states the slice-local
consequence execution may not silently alter; an **obligation** is a specific
state of affairs the slice must establish; a **verification criterion** states
what observation would demonstrate it; a **proof binding** states how that
criterion is presently established in this project; **execution evidence**
records the result at immutable Git state.

The trace is logical. It does not imply that each node needs a new numbered
corpus, that all edges are stored, or that any node lacks an owner today. Two
of its nodes are already owned and one is not:

- **obligation** — owned. A phase's `EX-N` exit criteria already are the
  obligations, with immutable ids, authored order, phase-qualified reference
  (`REQ-441`), and evolution lineage covering split, merge, and cross-phase
  relocation (`REQ-442`). The word itself is also taken: `DEC-101` and
  `src/design_run/runbook.rs` ship an **obligation** primitive — ordered runbook
  steps with per-step discharge — so a plan-level "obligation" would collide
  with live user-visible vocabulary under `STD-002`. This RFC uses the word for
  the *concept* and `EX-N` for the *record*; a future design proposing the word
  as a plan-level noun must argue past that collision first.
- **verification criterion** and **execution evidence** — owned, by the VT/VA/VH
  criterion rows and SPEC-002's Git-anchored coverage evidence respectively.
- **the edge between them** — **unowned**. No authored field links a `VT` row to
  the `EX` row it proves, and no coverage entry is bound to the digest of the
  check definition that produced it. Two independent studies reached this from
  opposite ends (see H9 and H13). It is the smallest real gap this trace
  exposes.

Doctrine should investigate both axes as explicit processes with distinct
authority and persistence rules. Whether they share stored structure is a
hypothesis, not an architectural commitment.

A plan is therefore not an infallible forecast. The proposed interpretation is
that it remains the current governing prediction of:

* authored intent;
* presently accepted knowledge;
* predicted execution;
* resolved semantic lineage.

Observed discovery and accumulated evidence remain adjacent typed inputs to
derived views and authorized reconciliation. They do not become governing plan
truth merely because a projection can display them together.

Determinism applies to the transitions, authority, provenance, and evidence. It does not require perfect foresight.

## Principles

### P1 — Predictions are authoritative but provisional

A selector, phase change declaration, or verification strategy states what is presently expected. It governs execution until revised, but its incompleteness is not itself misconduct.

A deviation becomes a governance problem when it is:

* forbidden;
* unauthorized;
* unexplained;
* deceptively represented;
* left unreconciled.

A previously unpredicted change with stated causal provenance is not equivalent
to an unexplained change. That provenance is itself a reviewable claim: it can be
incomplete, contradicted, or made stale by later evidence.

### P2 — Immutable history must not imply immutable active meaning

Immutable IDs should preserve historical identity and provenance. They should not force an obsolete statement to remain governing truth.

A criterion may be:

* refined;
* corrected;
* expanded;
* split;
* merged;
* withdrawn;
* replaced by another proof strategy.

These acts should create new immutable records and explicit lineage. The active plan is derived from the currently governing leaves of that lineage.

The invariant becomes:

> Records remain immutable; active semantics remain correctible.

### P3 — Semantic facts have one normative owner

A requirement, decision, assumption, invariant, or clarification should be stated normatively once.

Downstream records may express typed relations, source references, and local
consequences without restating the normative fact. Derived or materialized views
are permitted when their source and reconciliation rule are explicit:

```text
DEC-058
→ motivates OBJ-4
→ advanced by PHASE-03
→ implemented by CHG-17
→ verified by VT-41
→ revised by REC-18
```

This chain is a logical trace, not a proposed relation-label vocabulary or a
requirement that every node share one store. Plans, prompts, gates, and context
views should project typed canonical records rather than restate a decision as
independent truth.

### P4 — Relations carry meaning that paths cannot

A path selector can establish where a change was predicted. It cannot establish:

* what semantic change was intended;
* which requirement or decision authorized it;
* why an additional file became necessary;
* whether the drift was evolution, consequence, or negligence;
* which tests establish its correctness;
* what would be affected if its governing decision changed.

Path declarations remain the cheap, deterministic filesystem boundary for
confinement and Git conformance. Semantic records and relations should augment
that algebra where a proven consumer needs meaning that paths cannot carry; they
do not replace it.

### P5 — Observed discovery and authored truth remain separate

A worker may observe that the plan is incomplete. It must not thereby gain authority to rewrite the plan, requirement, decision, or selector registry.

SPEC-002 already establishes the required asymmetry: observed evidence may flow into judgement, while authored truth changes only through an explicit reconciliation act.

Progressive discovery should reuse that architecture:

```text
worker observation
→ structured proposal
→ derived drift
→ trusted classification by the control plane
→ authored revision, rejection, or redesign
```

No observation automatically becomes normative truth. A worker may propose a
classification and its evidence; it does not acquire authority to classify or
authorize the discovery merely by observing it.

### P6 — Discovery should be classified before it is judged

An undeclared delta should first receive a semantic classification such as:

* `discovered-dependency`;
* `generated-consequence`;
* `design-evolution`;
* `clarification`;
* `assumption-contradiction`;
* `incidental-damage`;
* `scope-escape`;
* `unknown`.

Classification does not imply acceptance. A class earns a durable vocabulary
entry only when it changes the review, authority, quarantine, or reconciliation
path. Until that evidence exists, `explained`, `unexplained`, and `forbidden`
are sufficient derived distinctions and richer kinds remain proposals.

### P7 — Enforcement should occur at the earliest actionable boundary

The same conformance relation may be evaluated at several points:

* plan authoring;
* dispatch preparation;
* worker checkpoint;
* result harvest;
* candidate admission;
* audit;
* close.

Each point has a different purpose. Early checks should allow the responsible actor to respond; later checks preserve independent verification.

RFC-023 proposed moving selector compatibility from close-time to plan-time and
commit-time; plan-time VT selector checks and a soft worker-commit signal now
partially implement that direction. The same principle should apply to the
remaining semantic omissions and discovered drift without claiming the structural
checks are absent.

### P8 — The core remains language-agnostic

Doctrine should own:

* semantic identity;
* relations;
* execution protocol;
* authority boundaries;
* Git anchors;
* evidence capture;
* lifecycle and result classification.

Projects should own language-specific procedures through declared adapters or commands.

Doctrine need not understand Rust, TypeScript, or Python semantics to establish that:

* a named procedure ran;
* it ran against a specific Git object;
* it failed before implementation;
* it passed after implementation;
* the evidence is fresh or stale;
* it is related to a particular requirement and change.

### P9 — Plans are authored views, not parallel truth

Human-readable phase plans remain useful. They should increasingly become
authored views over typed canonical records plus explicit phase-local consequences.
The logical trace may join several typed stores and consumer-specific projections;
RFC-003 and ADR-016 rule out treating every useful derived fact as a stored edge.

Adversarial planning passes should amend the surface that owns a fact or record a
finding against it. They should not produce several independently authoritative
accounts of the same intent.

### P10 — Proof strength should be proportional to risk

Not every criterion warrants multiple frontier-model turns.

Doctrine should prefer, in order:

1. deterministic structural checks;
2. executable project-declared checks;
3. independent test authorship;
4. adversarial implementation attack;
5. human semantic reconciliation.

Risk, blast radius, uncertainty, prior failure, and measured value should determine
escalation. RFC-026's weak evidence that expensive design review reduces later
defect discovery is a reason to measure the upper rungs rather than assume them.

### P11 — Actionability is a versioned interpretation of revisable dependencies

Added **2026-08-08**. It belongs conceptually beside P2 and P5 — immutable
records with correctible active meaning, and observed discovery that does not
confer authority — but principle numbers are append-only, so it sits here.

Missed implementation dependencies should be expected, not treated as
exceptional planning failure. The corpus bears this out: of 224 plans with git
history, **113 (50.4%) were revised after authoring**, across 197 post-authoring
plan commits, 47 of which name a specific `PHASE-NN` — work relocated between
phases, criteria retargeted mid-execution, phases split. Plan adaptation is the
normal case, not the exception.

Stable obligation identity should therefore not require immutable dependency
topology. A plan revision states the best-known semantic dependency relation at
that point in time; accepted discovery may add or remove dependency relations
and produce a new governing plan revision. Historical graph states remain
recoverable through Git and revision history. Individual dependency edges do not
require durable ids or lineage unless a future consumer demonstrates a need for
**edge** identity — which is a stronger claim than needing the edge itself, and
none is demonstrated today.

The invariant:

> Execution and evidence are evaluated against a coherent governing contract
> revision; later dependency correction changes current actionability without
> rewriting what was previously observed.

This is P1's provisional-prediction rule and P2's correctible-meaning rule
applied to dependency structure, and it is what makes H11 an ordinary discovery
path rather than a rewrite protocol.

**The gap this exposes is not the topology — it is the record.** Plan adaptation
is recorded but not queryable. It lives in git subject lines, `/consult`
transcripts, and notes prose; nothing in the plan model answers *which phases
were re-scoped after execution began, and why*. That deficiency is independent
of whether dependencies are ever stored, and would survive any repair to them.

## Goals

### G1 — Make progressive discovery safe and attributable

Workers and planners can record an unforeseen dependency or contradiction without silently changing authored scope and without immediately converting uncertainty into a human-blocking governance incident.

### G2 — Give discovered drift durable explanation and disposition

A discovered discrepancy can state its affected path or subject, phase and
activity provenance, observed Git state, factual evidence, requested action, and
eventual authorized disposition. A general semantic identity for every planned
change remains H3-dependent and is not a goal of the retained programme.

### G3 — Preserve correct active semantics

Obsolete gate conditions can be superseded, split, corrected, or withdrawn without rewriting history and without remaining falsely active.

### G4 — Reduce adversarial planning cost

Doctrine mechanically reports:

* unclaimed requirements, decisions, assumptions, or objectives;
* phase obligations with no predicted changes;
* changes with no verification;
* criteria that are vacuous at the base;
* unexplained path drift;
* stale or missing evidence.

Adversarial reviewers can then concentrate on semantic weakness rather than clerical cross-checking.

### G5 — Give confined workers a narrow challenge channel

A worker can report:

> This contract appears incomplete: this undeclared path was encountered while
> performing this activity, with this observed effect and evidence.

The worker cannot authorize the change or mutate authored Doctrine state.

### G6 — Establish end-to-end traceability

Doctrine can answer:

* Which tests verify this requirement?
* Are those tests still present and recently green?
* Which code changes implemented this decision?
* Why did this file enter the slice?
* Which requirements depend on this symbol or change?
* What is the likely blast radius if this decision changes?

### G7 — Accrete reusable context

Repository research performed during design, execution, testing, and review should enrich a durable map of relevant materials, relations, and provenance.

Subsequent agents should consume focused projections rather than repeatedly rediscovering the same context.

### G8 — Support adversarial red–green–refactor

The eventual execution model can prove:

```text
test authored independently
→ red at immutable Git state
→ implementation authored behind a write fence
→ green at immutable Git state
→ refactor retains green
```

RFC-023’s baton-passed loop remains a candidate. Reuse of the review ledger must
be tested against RFC-026's evidence and the information asymmetry the loop needs.

## Non-goals

This RFC does not propose:

* perfect prediction of implementation;
* a language-neutral semantic code analyser;
* automatic mutation of requirements from execution evidence;
* automatic promotion of a worker's proposed discovery class into authored truth;
* a generic workflow programming language;
* a universal repository graph or a single persistence schema for every trace;
* a new top-level entity for every conceptual node;
* adversarial TDD for every test;
* replacement of Git as the source of actual delta;
* replacement or duplication of SPEC-002's executable coverage seam;
* replacement of selectors as the authoritative conformance and candidate-admission contract;
* a guarantee that every material change can be predicted at file granularity;
* genericization of the RV ledger in the first tranche;
* settlement of the execution-capsule or Git integration architecture;
* immediate migration of every existing plan and criterion;
* a plan-level obligation ontology or stored dependency graph (H9, added
  2026-08-08);
* an arbitration subsystem, quorum protocol, or persisted arbitration state
  (H12, added 2026-08-08).

## Hypotheses

### H1 — Progressive concreteness and discovery can support one logical trace

Typed records and consumer-specific projections may support:

* planning completeness;
* worker context construction;
* selector projection;
* conformance;
* coverage;
* audit explanation;
* change-impact analysis.

This does not imply one stored graph. Progressive concreteness is authored
refinement; progressive discovery is an observed proposal followed by authorized
judgement. They have different actors, authority, persistence, and completeness
rules. The hypothesis is that their typed records can be joined into a coherent
trace without a new universal store or a proliferation of derivable relation
labels. It is falsified if the consumers require incompatible edge semantics or
if the joined view merely relocates semantic smear into relation maintenance.

### H2 — Selectors remain authoritative while semantic reads augment conformance

Selectors remain the authoritative declared-scope input to conformance and
candidate admission. They do not by themselves define physical write capability.
In the capsule model investigated by RFC-025 and SL-241, a worker may have broad
write authority inside its disposable checkout while the trusted control plane
applies selectors during harvest and admission. A selector-sourced write fence is
a distinct optional mechanism, not an implication of selector membership.

Before Stage 0, one candidate was to derive or enrich their long-term contents
from semantic change claims:

```text
change claim
→ material subject
→ selector projection
```

Stage 0 did not justify that additional owner. The existing
declared-versus-actual algebra remains valuable, and near-term improvements
should derive better reads from facts the repository already owns. ISS-251
captures the concrete plan-time omission case; IMP-310 owns the missing
selector-conformance technical specification. Neither requires a semantic
change-claim record.

`Derived` and `enriched` are not interchangeable. If a selector and change claim
disagree, the design must state which surface owns path authority before either
model ships. That question is now a re-entry condition for H3, not current design
work.

### H3 — A change claim may bridge design and Git — not supported by Stage 0

A candidate missing concept is an authored claim that some subject will be
created, modified, removed, or moved for a stated purpose.

It may begin as a phase-local row rather than a first-class numbered corpus. Its
identity should nevertheless be stable enough for its intended consumer and no
more durable than that consumer requires.

RFC-004 deliberately declined per-phase create/modify/delete declarations and a
richer change ontology until a proven consumer justified them. H3 therefore needs
a trial against selectors plus intent notes and improved read surfaces. It is
falsified if the new row does not find or explain consequential gaps materially
better, or if keeping it current costs more than the review and audit work it
removes.

EVD-004 records the completed comparison over SL-224 and SL-233. Thirteen
representative rows required 65 authored cells while `path`, `purpose`, and
`satisfies` repeated selectors, Git, objectives, criteria, requirements, or
implementation-time inference. `confidence` was wholly retrospective. The two
real undeclared paths in SL-233 PHASE-16 were identified and explained more
precisely by the existing conformance read; the reconstructed rows added no
earlier discovery or useful decision.

**Disposition:** the five-field authored row is falsified as RFC-027's default
bridge. It established no distinct normative fact and does not authorize schema
work or Stage 2. This does not falsify the broader semantic-continuity problem,
criterion lineage, or observed worker discovery. H3 may be reopened only by a
named consumer whose required fact is not already owned by selectors, plans,
criteria, entity relations, coverage, or Git, followed by a blind plan-time
trial. Adding fields to the rejected row is not evidence.

### H4 — Criterion supersession is a sufficient first repair

A complete gate ontology is unnecessary for the first increment.

Explicit supersession, disposition, and active-leaf derivation can stop obsolete
criteria from governing execution while preserving their history.

EVD-005 tested six criterion histories across SL-233, SL-241, and SL-224. The
positive cases required replacement, withdrawal, split, merge, and cross-phase
relocation; the unchanged control required no lineage act. Current prose and Git
preserved the histories, but a live-file reader sometimes needs commit order,
successor facts are restated across rows, and no derived read can distinguish an
active leaf from a tombstone or absorbed predecessor.

**Disposition:** retain H4 for specification coverage, narrowed to opt-in
criterion evolution across all criterion modes. Human and agent readers are real
consumers even where Rust does not interpret EN, EX, VA, or VH prose. The first
repair should own predecessor/successor disposition, derive governing leaves,
support one-to-many and many-to-one lineage plus relocation, and impose no record
on unchanged criteria. It need not mechanically prove that successor prose
preserves all prior meaning. VT's structured fields may support a later
set-conservation check, but that is an extension rather than the lineage
boundary.

### H5 — Worker discovery can remain in the observed tier

A worker-produced discovery proposal can be capsule-local or runtime state,
Git-anchored and harvested by the control plane before the disposable state is
removed.

It does not mutate the plan and does not carry an authoritative class. Trusted
machinery can present the worker's proposed class and evidence for authorized
classification and reconciliation. The durable observed sink, retention period,
and audit read path remain design questions.

### H6 — Plan criteria can reuse the executable coverage seam

SPEC-002 REQ-254–REQ-257 already establish a project-agnostic executable check
identity, real command execution, a production coverage write/withdraw path, and
Git-anchored observed results. The `verify` and `coverage verify` machinery is the
existing language-neutral primitive.

The hypothesis is that plan criteria can reference or project into that seam
without introducing a parallel command schema. Named per-test adapters can later
provide better ergonomics where supported. Base-versus-candidate vacuity and
red/green evidence are genuine additions; command execution itself is not.

### H7 — Adversarial TDD is a review protocol, not a separate engine

Test authorship, implementation, attack, disagreement, and disposition resemble the existing adversarial review ledger.

One possibility is a typed protocol over existing turn-taking, findings, and
dispositions rather than an independent orchestration subsystem. RFC-026's
non-convergence, correction-cost, and context-reset evidence means this is not yet
a leading implementation choice. Reuse must preserve RV's single-subject review
semantics and the information asymmetry needed by baton-passed TDD.

### H8 — The coverage substrate can anchor the chain

SPEC-002 keys coverage by requirement and contributing change, stores it slice-side, and Git-anchors the evidence.

Before Stage 0, a semantic change claim was a plausible identity for
`contributing_change`, allowing:

```text
requirement
→ change claim
→ verification criterion
→ execution evidence
```

without creating a competing coverage model. EVD-004 means this is not a current
design direction: no earned change-claim identity exists to project. Existing
coverage identities remain authoritative unless H3 is reopened with a distinct
fact, consumer, identity, and retention contract.

### H9 — Obligation-level dependency would improve actionability — tested, not supported

H9–H13 were added **2026-08-08** from an external design/proof pack. H9 and H13
were studied before they were entered, and are recorded with their results
rather than as open questions.

The candidate model was obligations as the smallest actionability unit, carrying
their own semantic dependencies inside phase envelopes:

```text
PHASE-03
  OBL-1
  OBL-2

PHASE-04
  OBL-3 needs PHASE-03.OBL-1
  OBL-4 after PHASE-03.OBL-2
```

with `needs` expressing a semantic correctness dependency, `after` a soft
sequencing preference, parallelism derived from the absence of blocking edges,
and phase-level blocked/actionable state derived from contained obligations
rather than authored.

**Disposition: not supported.** The obligation-graph study
(`obligation-study/conclusion.md`) tested it against the incumbent and against
three real slices, and its own kill condition — *most meaningful dependency is
genuinely phase-wide and obligation-level edges add only ceremony* — is met.
Three findings carry the result:

* **Fact ownership.** Eight candidate obligation fields were tested against
  RFC-003's derivability rule. Seven restate facts already owned: identity and
  order by `EX-N` (`REQ-441`), statement by `EX-N.text`, requirement links by
  the per-phase `requirements` array, refinement lineage by `REQ-442`, cycle and
  dangling-reference validation by `REQ-443`, phase actionability by
  `compute_next_phases()`. One is unowned, and it is not a dependency edge — see
  H13's R1.
* **An unchanged frontier.** Obligation granularity moved the actionable
  frontier on none of SL-233 (16 phases), SL-057, or SL-229. The single case
  where granularity differed — SL-233's PHASE-10 being runnable earlier — is an
  architectural deferral the plan states deliberately, not a coarseness artefact.
* **A second dependency implementation.** `src/dep_seq.rs` owns `needs`/`after`
  semantics at *entity* altitude and is kind-neutral but entity-bound; plan
  phases live at a different altitude with no bridge. Edges would mean a second
  implementation plus a naming collision with the shipped runbook obligation.

**What this disposition does not rest on.** An earlier draft also argued
dependency corrections are rare (3 tagged corrections in ~2,362 entrance
criteria). **That leg is withdrawn** — the instrument counted adherence to one
authoring convention in one field, and its markers sat in 4 of 225 plans, which
should itself have been the tell. The convention-independent re-measurement is
in P11: half of all plans are revised after authoring. The rejection stands on
fact ownership and the unchanged frontier, both independent of rate, but on a
narrower base than first written.

**Reopening condition.** A named consumer for which obligation granularity
*moves the actionable frontier* — plausibly a genuinely file-disjoint slice with
parallel obligations, which the three-slice sample did not contain. If one
appears, build it as the study's Model 4: graft slice-local nodes into the
existing `cordage` graph for the focus slice through a different loader and
store nothing new. That is verified cheap — `NodeId(u32)` is an opaque dense
index and `Projection<K: Copy + Ord>` is already generic, so the cost is a
widened key plus a loader arm, with no effect on corpus-wide loads. Cheap is not
a reason to build it; it is the shape to build *if* a consumer earns it.

### H10 — Recursive design can refine obligations rather than replacing the workflow

An obligation that proves insufficiently concrete during planning or
implementation may be refined into subordinate obligations and dependencies:

```text
OBL-17 implement parser migration

becomes, after discovery:

OBL-17a establish incumbent behaviour
OBL-17b settle compatibility rule
OBL-17c specify verifier
OBL-17d implement migration
```

**Narrowed by the same study.** The refinement *record* already has an owner:
`REQ-442` supplies replacement, withdrawal, one-to-many split, many-to-one
merge, and cross-phase relocation over criteria, with governing leaves derived
rather than duplicated. H10 therefore claims nothing new about storage.

What survives is the **epistemic** half: that the refinement may be produced by
re-entering the managed research / design / verification-planning workflow
against the smaller subject, rather than by a bespoke phase-local ritual. The
nested run may propose changes; it does not automatically acquire authority to
revise governing intent (P5). This is RFC-026's S7 — recursive design as a
property of the workflow — pointed at plan criteria.

*Confirm:* existing phase-local sketches and implementation discoveries can be
represented as `REQ-442` lineage acts produced by a nested run, with less
bespoke process and a smaller blocking radius than SL-233's four incompatible
sketch gates. *Kill:* nested runs need enough different lifecycle semantics that
reuse distorts both the slice-level design process and phase planning.

### H11 — Late dependency discovery can be reconciled transactionally

A worker may report:

```text
OBL-17 requires OBL-09 because <evidence>
observed against plan revision R7
```

The observed proposal does not mutate the canonical graph. The trusted control
plane or authorized reconciler may accept or reject it and, on acceptance,
derive revision R8 and recompute actionability. Work already running or complete
that R8 affects becomes **potentially stale and in need of revalidation** — not
automatically erased or rewritten. Whether prior evidence remains sufficient
should be derived from the exact dependency and criterion changes, not assumed
either way.

**Premise supported; mechanism untested.** The premise that late dependency
discovery is ordinary rather than exceptional is the P11 measurement: 50.4% of
plans revised after authoring, and SL-244 alone carrying nine plan commits,
several of them mid-execution retargets routed through `/consult`. The
transactional reconciliation *mechanism* has not been tried.

*Confirm:* the existing Git/revision and observed/authored seams provide
coherent snapshot semantics without a distributed mutable-DAG protocol. *Kill:*
accepted dependency correction routinely requires reconstructing so much
implicit state that obligation-level revisions cannot be evaluated locally.

*Do not inherit the withdrawn rarity claim* recorded under H9; it argued the
opposite of this measurement and was wrong.

### H12 — Most semantic escalations can be resolved below human authority

Worker escalation currently mixes two cases: decisions that genuinely require
the user, and decisions that merely exceed the worker's local context or
authority. The working model assumes three tiers:

```text
worker discretion
→ delegated semantic arbitration
→ human reserved authority
```

The control plane may route a decision case through progressively stronger
mechanisms:

```text
mechanical policy
→ independent semantic adjudicator
→ differentiated multi-agent arbitration
→ recursive research/design
→ human reserved authority
```

Differentiated roles are preferred to naive majority voting where the decision
benefits from separate epistemic functions — researcher (are the premises
true?), designer (which resolution serves governing intent?), verifier (is the
resolution falsifiable?), adversary (what breaks, what is unstated?), arbiter
(dispose or escalate). No quorum or protocol is proposed here.

The middle tier is not merely a cheaper escalation path. It should also receive
decisions workers currently take autonomously, where independent research,
verification, or adversarial judgement would materially improve the outcome — so
a useful mechanism reduces inappropriate human escalation and inappropriate
worker autonomy at once.

*Confirm:* historical implementation escalations can usually be decided from a
compact evidence-bearing case by independent agents given the right governing
context, with their authority bounded explicitly. *Kill:* the decisions
routinely depend on tacit user preferences or context that cannot be represented
in the governing design or policy without effectively recreating interactive
supervision.

RFC-026's **S8** carries the same three-tier premise from the review side and
its `OQ-4` defers the mechanism here; the two should not grow separate answers.

### H13 — Doctrine can address proof subjects without understanding source languages

Doctrine core owns criterion identity and revision, its relation to requirement
or obligation, proof mode, current binding, adapter identity and opaque subject,
resolution and execution outcome, Git anchors, red/green evidence where
selected, and attestation freshness. Projects and adapters own ecosystem
mechanics. The smallest useful adapter contract may be no more than:

```text
resolve(subject)
execute(subject)
```

with outcomes distinguishing at least `found`, `not-found`, `executed`,
`passed`, and `failed`. **Selecting zero tests must never satisfy an executable
criterion.** Later capabilities may enumerate or describe subjects, but core
should not parse Rust, Python, Go, or JavaScript test syntax to establish test
identity.

**Disposition: substantially already true — extend the SPEC-002 seam, do not
build a parallel model.** The proof-binding study
(`proof-binding-study/conclusion.md`) checked each property against the
incumbent. Criterion identity is already distinct from test locator
(`CoverageKey`'s 4-tuple versus the `VtCheck` recipe); a binding is already
revisable without criterion mutation (`check: Option<VtCheck>`, additive and
replaceable); the project-declared language-neutral command registry already
exists (`VerificationConfig` plus the pure `verify::resolve()`); VA/VH freshness
already differs from VT; core already operates on `Vec<String>` argv and holds
no test-topology concept at all.

**But H13 survives only if the matcher is unconditional.** There are two ways to
distinguish "ran zero tests" from "ran and passed": parse the runner's output —
which `src/regression.rs` INV-5 does, and which is exactly what H13 forbids in
core — or require a matcher, which is language-neutral. `coverage::valid`
currently exempts literal-command entries from the matcher requirement, so a
literal command that exits 0 having selected zero tests lands `Verified`,
flagged only as `[exit-code-only]` for a human to notice. Removing that
exemption resolves the tension without choosing: the project declares what
success looks like in its runner's output, and core stays neutral while
refusing to call an unmatched run green.

Four repairs are earned, and only the first two bear on the seam's semantics:

* **R1 — bind a coverage entry to its check definition's digest.** `check` is
  persisted verbatim and undigested, so swapping a matcher, command, or alias
  leaves the prior `Verified` unchallenged; entries go stale only when their
  *evidence's* Git anchor ages. The mechanism exists one subsystem over —
  `Step::material()` builds a netstring-framed, version-tagged digest over a
  step's whole definition, per `DEC-101`: *an id solves reference, not
  equivalence*. Positive control: `digest` appears 34× in `runbook.rs`, 0× in
  `coverage.rs`. This is the same defect as H9's unowned `EX`→`VT` link seen
  from the binding end — criterion and binding have no shared identity.
* **R2 — remove the literal-command exemption** from `coverage::valid`. No
  schema change, no new outcome variant, no language knowledge in core; the
  existing `MatcherRequired` refusal does the work. Its cost is bounded by
  however many existing entries rely on the exemption, which is a corpus query.
* **R3 — give `Unobtainable` its cause.** It fuses at least six conditions
  (resolve error, spawn failure, timeout, empty or unreadable glob match set,
  unparseable regex, empty argv) into `Blocked`, persisting no reason, exit
  code, or output, so a `Blocked` cell cannot be triaged without re-running it.
  `FailureSet::Unobtainable { why: String }` already carries its cause.
* **R4 — fill in `REQ-254`–`REQ-257`.** The four SPEC-002 requirements governing
  this seam are title-only: no statement, no acceptance criteria. Positive
  control: `REQ-113`–`REQ-115` in the same spec carry full statements and 3–6
  criteria each. R1 and R3 are schema changes to a persisted type and should be
  governed by the amendment R4 requires, so R4 precedes them. "Reuse the
  SPEC-002 seam" currently means reusing code governed by requirements that
  assert nothing.

**What the study did not establish**, stated so nothing more is read into it: no
cross-ecosystem probe was run, so language-neutrality rests on reading the code
rather than exercising it against pytest, Go, or Jest; the zero-test trace was
reasoned from `derive_status`, not executed; independent test authorship was not
investigated; and no adapter prototype was built, because the seam already
exists.

**One pattern recurs across both studies**, and it is the more useful finding
than either brief's proposal:

| Property | Has it | Lacks it |
|---|---|---|
| Definition-staleness via digest | `src/design_run/runbook.rs` | `src/coverage.rs` |
| Failure carrying its cause | `src/regression.rs` (`why: String`) | `src/coverage.rs` |
| Zero-result never reads as green | `src/regression.rs` (INV-5) | `src/coverage.rs` |
| Callee cannot self-declare success | `runbook.rs` (`Discharge::verified`) | `coverage.rs` |

The mechanisms exist and are good; they are unevenly distributed. Anyone
proposing new structure in this space should check the neighbouring subsystem
first — four for four, the answer was already in the tree.

## Threat and authority model

The first tranche targets omission, honest discovery, stale prediction, and
semantic duplication. It does not treat every undeclared path as hostile, and it
does not weaken the hard refusal of forbidden control-plane paths.

Three boundaries must remain explicit:

* The **capability boundary** determines what the execution environment can
  physically read or write. In RFC-025's leading capsule model, the disposable
  checkout may be broadly writable while canonical Git state, authored Doctrine
  state, credentials, unrelated host resources, and ungranted network services
  are absent or refused by construction. SL-241 is testing that claim; this RFC
  does not pre-judge its result.
* The **contract boundary** is the selector and phase-touch prediction used for
  ordinary conformance and candidate admission. An undeclared project path may be
  writable inside a capsule without thereby becoming accepted scope.
* The **reconciliation boundary** determines what an authorized actor may admit,
  reject, redesign, or fold into revised authored truth after execution shows the
  contract was incomplete. Acceptance must leave a durable disposition.

Stage 3's leading target is the clone/capsule authority model under RFC-025 and
SL-241. The current worktree funnel remains useful comparative and migration
evidence until the capsule probe settles its own claims.

- A **worker** may observe a discrepancy, retain it within its disposable
  capability boundary, and submit a Git-anchored factual report plus an optional
  proposed class. It cannot mutate authored Doctrine state, widen selectors,
  authorize the delta, or settle semantic truth.
- The **control plane** derives path drift, provisions or verifies the capability
  boundary, and presents proposals and evidence. Deterministic machinery may
  classify only mechanically defined cases; semantic classes require an
  authorized actor.
- An **authorized planner or reconciler** may revise the plan, reject the delta,
  direct a different implementation, or escalate to design. Acceptance must leave
  a durable authored or observed-to-authored trail appropriate to the governing
  capability.
- **Audit** independently checks both the delivered change and how discovery was
  handled. An explanation is evidence to evaluate, not proof by assertion.

**A candidate fourth actor, H12-dependent.** The actors above form two tiers:
the worker decides within its envelope, and everything else escalates to a
trusted human-backed side. A **delegated semantic arbiter** would sit between
them, receiving both what workers currently escalate unnecessarily and what they
currently decide unilaterally when independent judgement would have been better.
It is not part of the current authority model and must not be assumed by any
mechanism until H12 reports.

The submission it would receive is a **decision case**: the governing
obligation, the question or conflict, the relevant evidence, the affected
frontier, candidate options, and a recommendation if the worker has one. The
case is inspected and disposed on the trusted side. The worker does not open an
interactive human channel from its capsule, and does not acquire authority by
proposing the change — the same asymmetry P5 already imposes on discovery
proposals, applied to decisions.

## Candidate architectural implications

### 1. Conformance gains semantic explanation and disposition

The current differential remains:

```text
declared ∩ actual
declared \ actual
actual \ declared
```

Each cell can additionally distinguish:

```text
predicted and delivered
predicted but unnecessary
delivered as discovered dependency
delivered as generated consequence
delivered after design evolution
delivered but unexplained
forbidden delta
```

The deterministic conformance result remains path algebra. Its interpretation and
the subsequent control decision may additionally depend on explanation,
disposition, and authority; those semantics do not replace the algebra.

### 2. Undeclared work can be investigated without being accepted

A capsule or confined worker may be allowed to retain an undeclared local change while recording a discovery proposal.

That is not canonical acceptance.

The result can remain quarantined until the control plane:

* accepts the discovery into the active plan;
* directs a different implementation;
* rejects the change as damage or escape;
* escalates the design;
* requests human judgement.

Hard forbidden paths and control-plane state remain immediate refusals.

### 3. Gate conditions become lineages

A criterion record remains immutable. A later record may relate to it through:

```text
refines
corrects
expands
splits
merges
supersedes
withdraws
```

The governing criterion set is a derived view over active lineage leaves.

Evidence remains attached to the exact criterion revision it evaluated. Evidence for an obsolete criterion does not automatically satisfy its replacement.

### 4. Plans express confidence and uncertainty — H3-dependent

The rejected change row proposed distinguishing:

* required change;
* expected change;
* possible consequence;
* open implementation question;
* forbidden change.

Stage 0 did not show that storing these classes reduces speculative path
enumeration enough to justify their maintenance. This surface is parked with H3;
uncertainty should remain in its current owners until a consumer earns structure.

### 5. Audit evaluates the handling of discovery

Audit should not ask only whether the final delta matched the original prediction.

It should ask:

* Was the discovery recorded when encountered?
* Was the discrepancy and its claimed relevance plausibly evidenced?
* Did the worker remain within its capability boundary?
* Was authored intent revised by an authorized actor?
* Were affected criteria and context projections updated?
* Does the final implementation carry complete proof and provenance?

In a capsule that permits local commits and history rewriting, a local commit,
timestamp, or final proposal cannot by itself prove when the discrepancy was
first known. A requirement for contemporaneous discovery therefore needs a
trusted external ordering anchor: for example, a control-plane receipt followed
by inspection of the nominated capsule state, or an append-only event outside the
worker's rewritable authority. The capsule OID anchors content; it does not by
itself establish chronology.

### 6. Context becomes a derived projection

A worker context for one phase can be assembled from:

* claimed requirements and decisions;
* active criteria;
* phase objectives and selectors;
* material subjects reachable from existing authored relations;
* prior discoveries and reconciliations;
* related tests;
* current evidence freshness;
* unresolved questions;
* explicit forbidden surfaces.

The context map grows as design and execution reveal new relations.

### 7. Change impact becomes typed traversal

Changing a decision can surface:

* objectives and phases derived from it;
* active criteria interpreting or verifying it;
* files and symbols associated through selectors, coverage, or other existing
  authored relations;
* tests asserting its consequences;
* evidence made stale by its revision;
* prose projections likely to contain obsolete restatements.

Doctrine need not prove that every affected code location has been found. It can provide an increasingly useful, provenance-bearing blast-radius map.

### 8. Design projects an adaptation envelope

Added **2026-08-08**. The governing design should eventually project enough
information for execution to know what it may adapt:

```text
governing intent / invariants      reserved or high-authority
chosen realization                 provisional
known degrees of freedom           delegated
reserved decisions                 require named authority
capability / governance boundary   never locally negotiable
```

This is a conceptual output, not a proposed schema, and the last row is not a
projection of the same kind as the others: the capability boundary is enforced
by construction (RFC-025's capsule), not by a design statement, and appears here
only so a reader is not misled into thinking design can widen it.

Its purpose is to let execution adapt realization without silently changing
intent. Without the boundary, execution has two bad options — treat every
implementation discovery as a governance event, or let implementation quietly
alter intent. RFC-026's `G15` records the same absence from the review side, and
its `P9` proposes the design-artefact split that would produce this projection;
this RFC owns only what execution needs to consume.

The envelope is what a decision case (see *Threat and authority model*) is
evaluated against: a question is inside the worker's discretion, inside the
delegated tier, or reserved, according to which row it falls in. That makes the
envelope H12's precondition rather than an independent proposal — arbitration
with no stated envelope is unbounded by construction.

### Progressive proof binding

Added **2026-08-08**. A verification criterion should be able to exist before
its language- or project-specific proof procedure is known. The durable semantic
identity is the **criterion**; its **binding** may become more concrete during
design, planning, verifier authorship, or execution — the concreteness axis
applied to proof rather than to material change.

Conceptually three layers, not three new stores:

```text
criterion
  mode = executable | agent | human
  statement = semantic assertion

binding
  adapter = project-specific verifier
  subject = opaque verifier subject

observed evidence
  criterion revision
  binding identity
  Git anchor
  result / attestation
  freshness
```

Two rules follow, and they are the point of the separation:

* Changing a test name or a project runner revises the **binding** without
  touching the semantic criterion.
* Changing what must be proved is **criterion evolution** (see *Criterion
  lineage*) and must not inherit prior evidence automatically.

**Most of this is incumbent, and it is SPEC-002's.** `CoverageKey` already
carries criterion identity separately from the `VtCheck` recipe;
`check: Option<VtCheck>` is already additive and replaceable, so rebinding
without criterion mutation already works; `VerificationConfig` plus
`verify::resolve()` is already the project-declared, language-neutral command
registry. The layering above is a reading of that seam, not a replacement for
it, and H13 records the four repairs it needs.

The binding layer is where the missing identity bites twice over: nothing links
a criterion to the obligation it proves, and nothing binds an evidence row to
the *definition* of the check that produced it. Until a binding carries a
digest, "the criterion did not change" and "the proof did not change" are
indistinguishable to the engine, and only the second is currently checked.

### Executable procedure projection

A plan criterion should reference or project into SPEC-002's executable check
identity and verification configuration. The following illustrates the information
needed at the plan boundary, not a second command registry:

```toml
[criterion.procedure]
adapter = "project-command"
command = ["doctrine-project", "test-one", "coverage_cannot_mutate_status"]

[criterion.expect]
exit = 0
```

The engine records:

```text
criterion id
phase id
base oid
candidate oid
command identity
result
output digest
execution metadata
```

### Red–green evidence

```toml
[test_evidence]
criterion = "VT-029"
test_subject = "tests/reconcile.rs::coverage_cannot_mutate_status"
red_oid = "<test-only-oid>"
red_result = "assertion-failure"
green_oid = "<implementation-oid>"
green_result = "passed"
refactor_oid = "<optional-refactor-oid>"
```

The initial implementation should not attempt to semantically interpret arbitrary failure output. It may use coarse project-declared or adapter-declared admissible red classes.

### Derived views

The storage model should support derived views equivalent to:

```text
active criteria for phase
unclaimed design intent
plan-named paths outside slice selectors
criteria with no semantic target
criteria with no executable or attested proof
actual delta with no selector or reconciled explanation
discovery proposals awaiting reconciliation
requirement-to-evidence trace
decision-to-material impact
stale verification evidence
```

These are views, not independently authored truth.

## Authority and lifecycle

### Authored tier

Owned by trusted Doctrine operations:

* requirements;
* decisions, assumptions, questions, and invariants;
* active plan structure;
* criterion lineage;
* selector registry;
* reconciliation acts.

### Observed tier

Produced by execution and inspection:

* actual Git delta;
* discovery proposals;
* command results;
* red–green observations;
* worker history;
* verification output;
* coverage evidence;
* freshness and drift views.

### Reconciliation seam

A discovered claim may result in:

* **accept** — add or revise authored plan relations;
* **revise** — correct the governing requirement, decision, or criterion;
* **redesign** — return the slice to design;
* **reject** — remove the delta or replace the implementation;
* **defer** — preserve an explicit unresolved risk that continues to gate acceptance.

This extends the observe → reconcile → close topology without allowing observed state to author itself.

Keep observed conformance state separate from authorized disposition. Begin with:



A proposal remains unaccepted until an authorized disposition records why it may

Richer discovery classes should be added only when they route to different

RFC-025 permits workers to commit freely and treats capsule history as forensic

**Bounded-autonomy arbitration probe (folded from the pack's Stage C).** The
same seam carries decision cases, not only discovery proposals, and it should be
probed on the capsule architecture SL-241 has now exercised. The probe's
question: can a worker emit a compact decision case, block only the affected
frontier, continue unrelated work, and receive a trusted-side disposition
without any interactive capsule-to-human channel? That tests H12's cheapest
claim — that the *routing* works — and leaves the arbitration quality question
(which mechanism decides well) for a separate measurement. Its output is
evidence, not an arbitration subsystem: nothing here authorizes persisting
arbitration state, and open question 20 asks whether it needs durable identity
at all.

### Stage 4 — Proof projection and negative controls

Project active plan criteria into SPEC-002's existing executable verification
contract. Add only the missing proof semantics:

* execution against the exact base and candidate;
* vacuous-at-base classification;
* output identity or digest where required;
* red, green, and retained-after-refactor evidence keyed to the exact criterion
  revision and Git states.

Retain VT/VA/VH modes. Require negative-control evidence only where a criterion
explicitly selects that proof mode.

**Sequenced against H13's four repairs (folded from the pack's Stage B).** The
seam this stage projects into is governed by four title-only requirements, so
the ordering is forced rather than chosen:

1. **R4 first** — fill in `REQ-254`–`REQ-257`. Nothing below is
   conformance-checkable until the requirements governing the seam assert
   something.
2. **R2 next** — remove the literal-command matcher exemption. It closes a live
   false-green path (`exit 0` on zero selected tests reads as `Verified`), needs
   no schema change, and is a precondition of this stage's *own* negative-control
   claim: a vacuity check is worthless on a seam that treats an unmatched run as
   proof. Its cost is a corpus query — how many entries rely on the exemption.
3. **R1 and R3 after the amendment** — binding digest and `Unobtainable` cause
   are schema changes to a persisted type and should be governed by the SPEC-002
   amendment R4 produces.

Two arms of the proof-binding probe were **not** run and remain preconditions on
this stage rather than completed work: no cross-ecosystem exercise (pytest, Go,
Jest) exists, so P8's language-neutrality claim rests on reading the code; and
the zero-test trace was reasoned from `derive_status`, not executed. Run both
before treating H13's disposition as settled for a second ecosystem.

### Stage 5 — Deferred programmes

Adversarial TDD orchestration and context/impact projection remain separate later
programmes. Test RV-ledger reuse against RFC-026's evidence and the required
information asymmetry before choosing it. Build impact/context views only after
earlier stages produce useful typed facts; never begin with a universal repository
graph.

## Measures

Evaluate the direction by measuring:

* undeclared paths per slice;
* proportion explained by workers and proportion classified mechanically versus
  by an authorized actor;
* unexplained drift remaining at audit;
* plan findings discovered before dispatch;
* obsolete criteria still presented as active;
* criteria with executable evidence;
* criteria vacuous at the phase base;
* repeated repository research across agents;
* human interruptions per accepted phase;
* frontier-model tokens per accepted phase;
* audit findings attributable to semantic smear;
* time required to explain why a path changed;
* time required to assess the impact of revising a decision;
* authoring and maintenance cost of semantic change records;
* false confidence or stale metadata introduced by those records;
* improvement over selectors plus intent notes and better read surfaces.

The intended outcome is not fewer discoveries. It is earlier, cheaper, more precise handling of them.

## Open questions

1. **Parked with H3:** if new evidence reopens a change claim, is it a first-class entity, a phase-local record, or a relation-bearing facet shared by slices and coverage?
2. Does criterion lineage require a distinct stable lineage ID, or are supersession relations between immutable criterion IDs sufficient?
3. Which discovery classes may permit provisional continued work inside a capsule,
   and what externally anchored checkpoint must precede that continuation?
4. What authority can classify a discovery without human contact?
5. Should accepted discovery revise the original phase, create an explicit
   plan/selector amendment, or produce a specialized slice-governance disposition?
6. How should a decision revision mark downstream tests and evidence as stale?
7. **Parked with H3:** which material locators are worth structuring beyond paths?
8. Can the existing RV ledger carry the complete adversarial TDD protocol without distorting review semantics?
9. When does an implementation discovery become reusable project knowledge rather than slice-local history?
10. How should the cross-corpus relation surface evolve without creating a generic, weakly typed graph store?
11. **Provisionally answered by EVD-004:** the tested row owned no distinct fact.
    Reopening requires a consumer that demonstrates one.
12. **Parked with H3:** if reopened, are selectors derived from change claims or
    authoritative peers enriched by them, and which wins when they disagree?
13. Where does a worker discovery proposal survive after capsule/runtime cleanup,
    which classifications can be mechanical rather than semantic judgements, and
    what trusted anchor proves its ordering when capsule history is rewritable?
14. Is criterion immutability an invariant of IDs, rows, or authored history, and
    what are the active-leaf rules for split, merge, and withdrawal?

Questions 15–24 arrived with the 2026-08-08 pack. Four were answered by the two
studies before they were entered and are recorded with their answers, in the
same register as question 11.

15. **Answered by the obligation study:** what owns an obligation today is the
    phase's `EX-N` exit criteria — identity, order, and phase-qualified
    reference under `REQ-441`, refinement under `REQ-442`. No concept is
    missing at that node.
16. **Answered by the obligation study:** obligation ids are durable across
    refinement. `REQ-441` forbids renumbering and reuse; `REQ-442` represents
    refinement as lineage (replacement, withdrawal, split, merge, relocation)
    with governing leaves derived, not as identity churn.
17. What change to a dependency relation invalidates completion evidence, and
    what merely changes future scheduling? P11 states the invariant — evidence
    is never rewritten — but not the derivation rule that decides which
    completed work needs revalidation.
18. How should an in-flight worker at contract revision R react when R+1 changes
    a dependency in its ancestry? (H11.)
19. Which adaptation-envelope decisions are delegable by project policy, and
    which remain inherently human-reserved? (H12; RFC-026's `OQ-4` defers here.)
20. Does an arbitration case require durable identity, or can it remain observed
    or runtime state plus an authorized disposition — the same asymmetry
    question question 13 asks of discovery proposals?
21. Is proof binding part of the plan, part of coverage configuration, or a
    projection joining them? The layering exists conceptually; its owner does
    not.
22. **Answered by the proof-binding study:** the adapter protocol's trustworthy
    statement for `not-found` / zero selected tests is a **mandatory matcher**.
    An exit code is not a trustworthy statement, and the alternative — parsing
    runner output — is what P8 forbids in core. See H13's R2.
23. How much test topology (`unit`, `integration`, `system`, `acceptance`) needs
    machine semantics versus verifier guidance only? Core holds no topology
    concept today, so the current answer is *none*; the question is whether any
    consumer earns one.
24. **Partially answered by the proof-binding study:** the coverage freshness
    model cannot be reused *unchanged*. Freshness currently decays only with the
    evidence's Git anchor, so a rebinding leaves a stale `Verified` standing;
    attaching evidence to exact criterion revisions and bindings requires the
    binding digest of R1. What remains open is whether anything beyond that
    digest is needed.

## Provisional conclusion

The repository evidence supports continuing to investigate **progressive discovery
alongside progressive concreteness**. It does not yet establish a unified graph,
a change-claim schema, or the full programme as architectural direction.

Preserve:

* immutable records;
* explicit authority;
* selector conformance;
* worker confinement;
* Git-backed evidence;
* authored-versus-observed separation;
* default refusal of unreconciled drift.

The strongest working interpretation is:

> A plan is an authoritative statement of current knowledge, not a claim of complete foresight.

Stage 0 did not establish a semantic change record; EVD-004 closes that candidate
without closing the wider problem. EVD-005 retains opt-in criterion lineage
across all modes as the first bounded model repair. IMP-381 then established that
the whole phase-plan content model lacks a product and technical home, not merely
that lineage lacks one. IMP-382 carries the resulting `/spec-product` then
`/spec-tech` programme. ISS-251 and IMP-310 carry the selector/read-surface work
independently. Stage 3 should consume RFC-025 and SL-241's capsule evidence before
choosing its execution, checkpoint, ingestion, or disposition mechanism.

Two further prove-value studies reported on **2026-08-08** and their pattern is
the same as Stage 0's. An obligation graph was rejected — seven of eight
candidate fields restate owners that already exist, and granularity moved the
actionable frontier on none of three slices (H9). A criterion/binding/evidence
model was found to be substantially incumbent in SPEC-002 and needing four
bounded repairs rather than replacement (H13). Between them they leave exactly
one unowned fact — the link between a criterion and the obligation it proves,
and between an evidence row and the check definition that produced it. Both
studies found it independently, from opposite ends, and it is a field on an
existing row.

The generalisation is worth more than either verdict: **the mechanisms exist and
are unevenly distributed.** Definition-staleness by digest, failures that carry
their cause, and a zero result that can never read as green are all solved in
one subsystem and absent from its neighbour. Before proposing new structure in
this space, check the neighbouring subsystem — four for four, the answer was
already in the tree.

If later hypotheses survive, a Revision/ADR and product/technical specs can
ratify the durable architecture. The longer-term aspiration remains to make every
accepted deviation explainable, every governing semantic statement singular, and
every requirement traceable through design, change, test, execution, and current
evidence.
