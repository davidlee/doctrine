# RFC-027: Progressive discovery and proof-bearing plans

> Status: **open** — architectural direction and incremental implementation guide.
> This RFC proposes principles, hypotheses, and candidate design surfaces. It does not settle a final schema or supersede RFC-004, RFC-023, SPEC-002, or the orchestration work currently under review.

## Context

Doctrine’s selectors, plans, phase gates, execution controls, and coverage machinery are converging on an end-to-end chain of proof:

```text
requirement
→ design intent
→ phase obligation
→ intended change
→ assertion
→ execution evidence
→ requirement coverage
```

The existing pieces already demonstrate substantial value:

* selectors distinguish scope-relevant, design-target, and actual-delta paths;
* Git supplies immutable evidence of what changed;
* phase gates can constrain completion;
* coverage can relate evidence to requirements;
* reconciliation separates observed reality from authored truth;
* confinement prevents workers from silently extending their own authority.

The current selector model nevertheless carries only path, intent, and an optional note. Its principal derived view is the declared-versus-actual path differential. This is effective at finding divergence, but not at explaining it.

RFC-023 identifies several consequences:

* behavioural gates may currently be satisfied by textual presence;
* required file touches are discovered manually;
* undeclared paths are surfaced when the responsible worker cannot repair the plan;
* objectives may have no owning phase;
* immutable criterion IDs become tombstones when their purpose changes.
  These are not independent defects. They arise from a shared assumption:

> The plan is treated as a complete declaration of future execution, while execution is treated primarily as evidence of conformance or violation.

Real implementation is also a process of discovery. Dependencies emerge, assumptions fail, secondary effects become visible, assertions prove inadequate, and designs become more concrete. Doctrine currently represents progressive concreteness weakly and progressive discovery mostly as drift.

The result is expensive in several ways:

1. An overlooked file touch is treated as a governance violation before Doctrine can distinguish discovery from negligence.
2. Path drift carries insufficient semantic metadata to explain why it occurred.
3. Robust plans require repeated adversarial prose review and manual cross-checking.
4. confined workers can identify a defective contract but have little structured ability to challenge it.
5. a realization expressed redundantly across plans, prose, gates, and selectors must be corrected in every copy.
6. immutable gate identities preserve history by leaving semantically incorrect criteria active, neutered, or disguised as tombstones.

## Thesis

Planning should advance along two independent axes:

```text
progressive concreteness

requirement
→ objective or obligation
→ phase responsibility
→ intended change
→ assertion
→ executable procedure
→ evidence
```

```text
progressive discovery

predicted
→ questioned
→ discovered
→ classified
→ authorized or rejected
→ reconciled
```

Doctrine should model both as first-class processes.

A plan is therefore not an infallible contract. It is the current governing projection of:

* authored intent;
* presently accepted knowledge;
* predicted execution;
* observed discovery;
* resolved semantic lineage;
* accumulated evidence.

Determinism applies to the transitions, authority, provenance, and evidence. It does not require perfect foresight.

## Principles

### P1 — Predictions are authoritative but provisional

A selector, phase change declaration, or verification strategy states what is presently expected. It governs execution until revised, but its incompleteness is not itself misconduct.

A deviation becomes a governance problem when it is:

* forbidden;
* unauthorized;
* unexplained;
* deceptively represented;
* left unreconciled.

A previously unpredicted change with complete causal provenance is not equivalent to an unexplained change.

### P2 — Immutable history must not imply immutable active meaning

Immutable IDs should preserve historical identity and provenance. They should not force an obsolete statement to remain governing truth.

A criterion may be:

* refined;
* corrected;
* expanded;
* split;
* merged;
* withdrawn;
* replaced by another proof strategy.

These acts should create new immutable records and explicit lineage. The active plan is derived from the currently governing leaves of that lineage.

The invariant becomes:

> Records remain immutable; active semantics remain correctible.

### P3 — Semantic facts have one owner

A requirement, decision, assumption, invariant, or clarification should be stated normatively once.

Downstream records should express relations and local consequences:

```text
DEC-058
→ motivates OBJ-4
→ advanced by PHASE-03
→ implemented by CHG-17
→ verified by VT-41
→ revised by REC-18
```

Plans, prompts, gates, and context views should project this graph rather than restate the decision independently.

### P4 — Relations carry meaning that paths cannot

A path selector can establish where a change was predicted. It cannot establish:

* what semantic change was intended;
* which requirement or decision authorized it;
* why an additional file became necessary;
* whether the drift was evolution, consequence, or negligence;
* which tests establish its correctness;
* what would be affected if its governing decision changed.

Path declarations should remain as useful projections, but semantic reasoning should operate on relational change claims and evidence.

### P5 — Observed discovery and authored truth remain separate

A worker may observe that the plan is incomplete. It must not thereby gain authority to rewrite the plan, requirement, decision, or selector registry.

SPEC-002 already establishes the required asymmetry: observed evidence may flow into judgement, while authored truth changes only through an explicit reconciliation act.

Progressive discovery should reuse that architecture:

```text
worker observation
→ structured proposal
→ derived drift
→ trusted classification
→ authored revision, rejection, or redesign
```

No observation automatically becomes normative truth.

### P6 — Discovery should be classified before it is judged

An undeclared delta should first receive a semantic classification such as:

* `discovered-dependency`;
* `generated-consequence`;
* `design-evolution`;
* `clarification`;
* `assumption-contradiction`;
* `incidental-damage`;
* `scope-escape`;
* `unknown`.

Classification does not imply acceptance. It determines the appropriate review, authority, and reconciliation path.

### P7 — Enforcement should occur at the earliest actionable boundary

The same conformance relation may be evaluated at several points:

* plan authoring;
* dispatch preparation;
* worker checkpoint;
* result harvest;
* candidate admission;
* audit;
* close.

Each point has a different purpose. Early checks should allow the responsible actor to respond; later checks preserve independent verification.

RFC-023 already proposes moving selector compatibility from close-time to plan-time and commit-time. The same principle should apply to semantic omissions and discovered drift.

### P8 — The core remains language-agnostic

Doctrine should own:

* semantic identity;
* relations;
* execution protocol;
* authority boundaries;
* Git anchors;
* evidence capture;
* lifecycle and result classification.

Projects should own language-specific procedures through declared adapters or commands.

Doctrine need not understand Rust, TypeScript, or Python semantics to establish that:

* a named procedure ran;
* it ran against a specific Git object;
* it failed before implementation;
* it passed after implementation;
* the evidence is fresh or stale;
* it is related to a particular requirement and change.

### P9 — Plans are graph projections, not parallel truth

Human-readable phase plans remain useful. They should increasingly become projections of structured semantic records and relations.

Adversarial planning passes should add findings, relations, refinements, and dispositions to one model. They should not produce several independently maintained accounts of the same intent.

### P10 — Proof strength should be proportional to risk

Not every criterion warrants multiple frontier-model turns.

Doctrine should prefer, in order:

1. deterministic structural checks;
2. executable project-declared checks;
3. independent test authorship;
4. adversarial implementation attack;
5. human semantic reconciliation.

Risk, blast radius, uncertainty, and prior failure should determine escalation.

## Goals

### G1 — Make progressive discovery safe and attributable

Workers and planners can record an unforeseen dependency or contradiction without silently changing authored scope and without immediately converting uncertainty into a human-blocking governance incident.

### G2 — Give changes semantic identity

A planned or discovered change can state:

* its subject;
* its operation;
* its purpose;
* its authority;
* its phase;
* its causal predecessor;
* its expected evidence;
* its eventual Git realization.

### G3 — Preserve correct active semantics

Obsolete gate conditions can be superseded, split, corrected, or withdrawn without rewriting history and without remaining falsely active.

### G4 — Reduce adversarial planning cost

Doctrine mechanically reports:

* unclaimed requirements, decisions, assumptions, or objectives;
* phase obligations with no predicted changes;
* changes with no verification;
* criteria that are vacuous at the base;
* unexplained path drift;
* stale or missing evidence.

Adversarial reviewers can then concentrate on semantic weakness rather than clerical cross-checking.

### G5 — Give confined workers a narrow challenge channel

A worker can report:

> This contract requires an undeclared change, for this reason, in support of this existing obligation.

The worker cannot authorize the change or mutate authored Doctrine state.

### G6 — Establish end-to-end traceability

Doctrine can answer:

* Which tests verify this requirement?
* Are those tests still present and recently green?
* Which code changes implemented this decision?
* Why did this file enter the slice?
* Which requirements depend on this symbol or change?
* What is the likely blast radius if this decision changes?

### G7 — Accrete reusable context

Repository research performed during design, execution, testing, and review should enrich a durable map of relevant materials, relations, and provenance.

Subsequent agents should consume focused projections rather than repeatedly rediscovering the same context.

### G8 — Support adversarial red–green–refactor

The eventual execution model can prove:

```text
test authored independently
→ red at immutable Git state
→ implementation authored behind a write fence
→ green at immutable Git state
→ refactor retains green
```

RFC-023’s baton-passed loop and its possible reuse of the review ledger remain the leading direction.

## Non-goals

This RFC does not propose:

* perfect prediction of implementation;
* a language-neutral semantic code analyser;
* automatic mutation of requirements from execution evidence;
* a generic workflow programming language;
* a new top-level entity for every conceptual node;
* adversarial TDD for every test;
* replacement of Git as the source of actual delta;
* settlement of the execution-capsule or Git integration architecture;
* immediate migration of every existing plan and criterion.

## Hypotheses

### H1 — Progressive concreteness and progressive discovery can share one graph

The same relations can support:

* planning completeness;
* worker context construction;
* selector projection;
* conformance;
* coverage;
* audit explanation;
* change-impact analysis.

Separate bespoke registries would reproduce the current smear.

### H2 — Selectors become projections, not obsolete machinery

Selectors remain the practical filesystem boundary used by confinement and Git conformance.

Their long-term contents can be derived or enriched from semantic change claims:

```text
change claim
→ material subject
→ selector projection
```

The existing declared-versus-actual algebra remains valuable. Its result gains semantic interpretation rather than being replaced.

### H3 — A change claim is the likely bridge between design and Git

The missing concept is an authored claim that some subject will be created, modified, removed, or moved for a stated purpose.

It may begin as a phase-local row rather than a first-class numbered corpus. Its identity should nevertheless be stable enough to receive relations and evidence.

### H4 — Criterion supersession is a sufficient first repair

A complete gate ontology is unnecessary for the first increment.

Explicit supersession, disposition, and active-leaf derivation can stop obsolete criteria from governing execution while preserving their history.

### H5 — Worker discovery can remain in the observed tier

A worker-produced discovery record can be capsule-local or runtime state, Git-anchored and harvested by the control plane.

It need not mutate the plan. Trusted machinery can present it for classification and reconciliation.

### H6 — Generic executable procedures provide the language-neutral seam

A project-declared command plus expected outcome is sufficient as the universal primitive.

Named per-test adapters can later provide better ergonomics where supported. RFC-023 already identifies executable VA rows and per-test execution as overlapping forms of this seam.

### H7 — Adversarial TDD is a review protocol, not a separate engine

Test authorship, implementation, attack, disagreement, and disposition resemble the existing adversarial review ledger.

The likely implementation is a new typed protocol over existing turn-taking, findings, and dispositions rather than an independent orchestration subsystem.

### H8 — The coverage substrate can anchor the chain

SPEC-002 keys coverage by requirement and contributing change, stores it slice-side, and Git-anchors the evidence.

A semantic change claim is a plausible identity for `contributing_change`, allowing:

```text
requirement
→ change claim
→ verification criterion
→ execution evidence
```

without creating a competing coverage model.

## Architectural implications

### 1. Conformance becomes semantic

The current differential remains:

```text
declared ∩ actual
declared \ actual
actual \ declared
```

Each cell can additionally distinguish:

```text
predicted and delivered
predicted but unnecessary
delivered as discovered dependency
delivered as generated consequence
delivered after design evolution
delivered but unexplained
forbidden delta
```

The control decision should depend on semantic class and authority, not path membership alone.

### 2. Undeclared work can be investigated without being accepted

A capsule or confined worker may be allowed to retain an undeclared local change while recording a discovery proposal.

That is not canonical acceptance.

The result can remain quarantined until the control plane:

* accepts the discovery into the active plan;
* directs a different implementation;
* rejects the change as damage or escape;
* escalates the design;
* requests human judgement.

Hard forbidden paths and control-plane state remain immediate refusals.

### 3. Gate conditions become lineages

A criterion record remains immutable. A later record may relate to it through:

```text
refines
corrects
expands
splits
merges
supersedes
withdraws
```

The governing criterion set is a derived view over active lineage leaves.

Evidence remains attached to the exact criterion revision it evaluated. Evidence for an obsolete criterion does not automatically satisfy its replacement.

### 4. Plans express confidence and uncertainty

A phase should be able to distinguish:

* required change;
* expected change;
* possible consequence;
* open implementation question;
* forbidden change.

This reduces speculative path enumeration while retaining useful expectations.

### 5. Audit evaluates the handling of discovery

Audit should not ask only whether the final delta matched the original prediction.

It should ask:

* Was the discovery recorded when encountered?
* Was its causal relation plausible and evidenced?
* Did the worker remain within provisional authority?
* Was authored intent revised by an authorized actor?
* Were affected criteria and context projections updated?
* Does the final implementation carry complete proof and provenance?

### 6. Context becomes a derived projection

A worker context for one phase can be assembled from:

* claimed requirements and decisions;
* active criteria;
* predicted change claims;
* relevant material subjects;
* prior discoveries and reconciliations;
* related tests;
* current evidence freshness;
* unresolved questions;
* explicit forbidden surfaces.

The context map grows as design and execution reveal new relations.

### 7. Change impact becomes graph traversal

Changing a decision can surface:

* objectives and phases derived from it;
* change claims implementing it;
* files and symbols associated with those claims;
* tests asserting its consequences;
* evidence made stale by its revision;
* prose projections likely to contain obsolete restatements.

Doctrine need not prove that every affected code location has been found. It can provide an increasingly useful, provenance-bearing blast-radius map.

## Candidate design surface

The shapes in this section are illustrative. They establish responsibilities rather than final TOML.

### Phase change claim

```toml
[[phase.change]]
id = "CHG-P03-01"
subject.path = "src/reconcile.rs"
subject.locator = "reconcile"
operation = "modify"
purpose = "Make the reconcile writer the sole authored-status mutation path."
satisfies = ["REQ-105", "DEC-058"]
confidence = "required"
```

Candidate operations:

```text
create
modify
remove
move
```

Candidate confidence classes:

```text
required
expected
possible
```

A first implementation may restrict the subject to paths. Locators can remain opaque strings until another consumer justifies structure.

### Discovered change proposal

```toml
[[discovery]]
id = "DISC-P03-02"
kind = "generated-consequence"
subject.path = "tests/help_output.rs"
operation = "modify"
caused_by = "CHG-P03-01"
supports = ["REQ-105"]
note = "Adding the command changes the checked help golden."
git_anchor = "<capsule-oid>"
```

This record belongs initially to observed runtime state. It does not rewrite `phase.change` or the selector list.

### Criterion lineage

```toml
[[criterion]]
id = "VT-029"
satisfies = ["REQ-105"]
supersedes = ["VT-017"]
change_kind = "correction"
statement = "Coverage updates cannot mutate authored requirement status."
```

Possible change kinds:

```text
refinement
correction
expansion
split
merge
replacement
withdrawal
```

Derived checks should reject:

* supersession cycles;
* missing predecessors;
* active criteria superseded by active descendants;
* split or merge relations with malformed cardinality;
* phases with no determinable active criterion order.

### Executable procedure

```toml
[criterion.procedure]
adapter = "project-command"
command = ["doctrine-project", "test-one", "coverage_cannot_mutate_status"]

[criterion.expect]
exit = 0
```

The engine records:

```text
criterion id
phase id
base oid
candidate oid
command identity
result
output digest
execution metadata
```

### Red–green evidence

```toml
[test_evidence]
criterion = "VT-029"
test_subject = "tests/reconcile.rs::coverage_cannot_mutate_status"
red_oid = "<test-only-oid>"
red_result = "assertion-failure"
green_oid = "<implementation-oid>"
green_result = "passed"
refactor_oid = "<optional-refactor-oid>"
```

The initial implementation should not attempt to semantically interpret arbitrary failure output. It may use coarse project-declared or adapter-declared admissible red classes.

### Derived views

The storage model should support derived views equivalent to:

```text
active criteria for phase
unclaimed design intent
phase changes outside slice selectors
change claims with no criterion
criteria with no executable or attested proof
actual delta with no predicted or discovered claim
discovered claims awaiting reconciliation
requirement-to-evidence trace
decision-to-material impact
stale verification evidence
```

These are views, not independently authored truth.

## Authority and lifecycle

### Authored tier

Owned by trusted Doctrine operations:

* requirements;
* decisions, assumptions, questions, and invariants;
* active plan structure;
* phase change claims;
* criterion lineage;
* selector registry;
* reconciliation acts.

### Observed tier

Produced by execution and inspection:

* actual Git delta;
* discovery proposals;
* command results;
* red–green observations;
* worker history;
* verification output;
* coverage evidence;
* freshness and drift views.

### Reconciliation seam

A discovered claim may result in:

* **accept** — add or revise authored plan relations;
* **revise** — correct the governing requirement, decision, criterion, or change claim;
* **redesign** — return the slice to design;
* **reject** — remove the delta or replace the implementation;
* **defer** — preserve an explicit unresolved risk that continues to gate acceptance.

This extends the observe → reconcile → close topology without allowing observed state to author itself.

## Long-term objectives

The mature system should support queries resembling:

```text
doctrine trace REQ-105
doctrine coverage show REQ-105 --live
doctrine impact DEC-058
doctrine explain path tests/help_output.rs --slice SL-233
doctrine context PHASE-03
doctrine plan gaps SL-233
doctrine discoveries SL-233 --unreconciled
```

A requirement trace should be able to show:

```text
REQ-105
→ interpreted by DEC-058
→ advanced by PHASE-03
→ implemented by CHG-P03-01
→ verified by VT-029
→ red at T
→ green at G
→ retained after refactor R
→ current evidence fresh at HEAD
```

A decision impact view should show known downstream relations without claiming semantic completeness:

```text
DEC-058
→ 2 requirements
→ 3 active criteria
→ 4 change claims
→ 7 material subjects
→ 6 tests
→ 1 stale evidence cell
```

The primary product objective is not a perfectly modelled repository. It is a compounding reduction in the cost of:

* acquiring relevant context;
* constructing robust plans;
* explaining drift;
* verifying implementation;
* revising decisions;
* auditing delivered work.

## Sequencing

### Stage 1 — Repair criterion evolution

Implement RFC-023 Direction H before broadening the ontology:

* `supersedes` or equivalent lineage relation;
* explicit criterion disposition;
* active-leaf derivation;
* dimmed historical rendering;
* cycle and reference validation;
* explicit phase order.

This removes the immediate pressure to preserve incorrect active semantics.

### Stage 2 — Trace intent into the plan

Add `satisfies` relations from criteria and phases to existing linked records.

Report:

* linked design records claimed by no phase;
* phase claims with no active criterion;
* active criteria satisfying no declared intent.

Do not introduce a new objective entity until existing DEC, QUE, ASM, INV, requirement, and phase relations prove insufficient.

### Stage 3 — Add semantic phase change claims

Introduce optional phase-local change rows containing:

* stable local ID;
* path;
* operation;
* purpose;
* `satisfies`;
* confidence.

Derive the phase touch set from these rows and compare it with slice selectors during `check plan`.

Keep existing selector behaviour intact.

### Stage 4 — Add typed discovery records

Give workers a narrow runtime command or artifact for proposing unexpected changes.

The minimum record should carry:

* subject path;
* intended operation;
* discovery class;
* causal predecessor or governing obligation;
* explanation;
* Git anchor.

At harvest, classify actual paths as:

```text
predicted
explained-discovered
unexplained
forbidden
```

Only `forbidden` is necessarily an immediate security failure. `explained-discovered` remains unaccepted until reconciled.

### Stage 5 — Introduce executable generic criteria

Add the universal project-command procedure:

* command;
* expected exit;
* exact candidate execution;
* output capture or digest;
* Git anchoring.

Add vacuity evaluation against the phase base.

Retain current VT/VA/VH modes while allowing executable procedures to strengthen VT and VA.

### Stage 6 — Persist negative-control evidence

Record red and green observations durably.

Initially this may be a small evidence structure attached to the phase or coverage entry. Do not wait for the full adversarial orchestration model.

Require red evidence only where a criterion explicitly selects a TDD proof mode.

### Stage 7 — Reuse review orchestration for adversarial TDD

Model test-author, implementer, attacker, and adjudicator as typed review postures.

Add:

* role-specific context projections;
* write fences;
* red acceptance;
* implementation handoff;
* additional-test findings;
* disposition and escalation.

Route only selected high-risk criteria through this protocol.

### Stage 8 — Generalize the context and impact graph

Once change claims, discoveries, and executable evidence produce useful relations:

* add context projections;
* add path explanation;
* add decision and requirement impact views;
* retain discovered material relations for future slices;
* surface stale evidence when upstream authority changes.

Do not begin by constructing a universal repository graph. Grow it from relations with proven consumers.

## Near-term incremental changes

The first implementation programme should remain small enough to proceed while orchestration is unsettled.

### Increment 1 — Criterion supersession

Deliver:

* `superseded_by` or `supersedes`;
* an inactive/superseded derived status;
* `slice show` rendering that distinguishes governing and historical criteria;
* validation for missing targets and cycles;
* explicit phase order.

No execution semantics need change.

### Increment 2 — Intent coverage

Deliver:

* `satisfies` on criteria;
* a derived report of linked records with no active criterion;
* a report of active criteria with no semantic target;
* integration into `check plan`.

This directly replaces the manual scope-to-plan sweep identified in RFC-023.

### Increment 3 — Phase change metadata

Replace or augment a bare `touches` proposal with:

```text
path
operation
purpose
confidence
satisfies
```

Deliver:

* selector compatibility at plan time;
* phase touch-set projection;
* missing-purpose and missing-authority lints;
* no enforcement change at worker commit yet.

### Increment 4 — Discovery proposal and typed drift

Add a capsule-local or runtime discovery artifact and harvest it with the result.

Change conformance output from:

```text
undeclared
```

to:

```text
undeclared-unexplained
undeclared-discovered
forbidden
```

Do not automatically mutate selectors or plans.

This is the first increment that materially reduces the cost of imperfect foresight.

### Increment 5 — Executable VA and vacuity

Implement:

* `command`;
* `expect_exit`;
* execution against base and candidate;
* vacuous-at-base verdict;
* Git-anchored result evidence.

This delivers substantial verification value without committing to per-language test discovery.

### Increment 6 — Red evidence

Add a durable red observation keyed to an active criterion and test subject.

This prepares the evidence model for adversarial TDD without requiring multiple agents yet.

## Measures

Evaluate the direction by measuring:

* undeclared paths per slice;
* proportion classified without human intervention;
* unexplained drift remaining at audit;
* plan findings discovered before dispatch;
* obsolete criteria still presented as active;
* criteria with executable evidence;
* criteria vacuous at the phase base;
* repeated repository research across agents;
* human interruptions per accepted phase;
* frontier-model tokens per accepted phase;
* audit findings attributable to semantic smear;
* time required to explain why a path changed;
* time required to assess the impact of revising a decision.

The intended outcome is not fewer discoveries. It is earlier, cheaper, more precise handling of them.

## Open questions

1. Is a change claim ultimately a first-class entity, a phase-local record, or a relation-bearing facet shared by slices and coverage?
2. Does criterion lineage require a distinct stable lineage ID, or are supersession relations between immutable criterion IDs sufficient?
3. Which discovery classes may permit provisional continued work inside a capsule?
4. What authority can classify a discovery without human contact?
5. Should accepted discovery revise the original phase, create an explicit amendment, or produce a reconciliation record only?
6. How should a decision revision mark downstream tests and evidence as stale?
7. Which material locators are worth structuring beyond paths?
8. Can the existing RV ledger carry the complete adversarial TDD protocol without distorting review semantics?
9. When does an implementation discovery become reusable project knowledge rather than slice-local history?
10. How should the cross-corpus relation surface evolve without creating a generic, weakly typed graph store?

## Outcome

Adopt **progressive discovery alongside progressive concreteness** as the architectural direction for selectors, phase gates, execution evidence, and context construction.

Preserve:

* immutable records;
* explicit authority;
* selector conformance;
* worker confinement;
* Git-backed evidence;
* authored-versus-observed separation;
* default refusal of unreconciled drift.

Change the interpretation:

> A plan is an authoritative statement of current knowledge, not a claim of complete foresight.

The near-term work should establish criterion supersession, semantic intent relations, phase change metadata, and typed discovery before attempting the full adversarial TDD or repository-impact graph.

The long-term system should make every accepted deviation explainable, every governing semantic statement singular, and every requirement traceable through design, change, test, execution, and current evidence.
