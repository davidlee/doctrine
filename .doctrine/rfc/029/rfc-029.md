# RFC-029: Proof binding and the plan surface

**Status:** open. **Carved out of RFC-027 on 2026-08-08.** Governance-neutral
deliberation; asserts no canon (ADR-014).

## Context

Doctrine cannot answer two questions about its own plans:

> *What proves this obligation?* and *why did this proof fail?*

Both are answerable in principle — the data exists, the mechanisms exist, and in
three cases they exist in a neighbouring module. They are not answerable in
practice, because the field that would connect a criterion to its proof was
never authored, the evidence is not bound to the check that produced it, the
failure discards its own cause, and the requirements governing the seam assert
nothing.

This RFC exists because that cluster was buried. It was discovered inside
RFC-027 — *Progressive discovery and proof-bearing plans* — which is a 1900-line
deliberation spanning discovery proposals, conformance drift, execution capsules,
worker authority and bounded autonomy. The proof cluster is separable from all of
it, has a different shape (five concrete repairs with a forced order, not an open
architectural question), and is currently gated on a specification nobody has
written. Tracking it inside RFC-027 means re-reading the capsule authority model
to find out whether a coverage entry carries a digest.

### Where this came from

Two prove-value studies reported on 2026-08-08, commissioned from an external
design/proof pack. Both set out to add an ontology. **Both concluded no.**

* The **obligation study** rejected a plan-level obligation graph: seven of eight
  candidate fields restated owners that already existed, and obligation
  granularity moved the actionable frontier on none of three real slices.
* The **proof-binding study** found a criterion/binding/evidence model
  substantially *incumbent* in SPEC-002, needing four bounded repairs rather than
  replacement.

Between them they left **exactly one unowned fact**, and both found it
independently from opposite ends. That convergence is the strongest signal
either study produced and it is the spine of this RFC.

### The governing generalisation

RFC-027 states it and this RFC inherits it:

> **The mechanisms exist. They are unevenly distributed.**

| Property | Has it | Lacks it |
|---|---|---|
| Definition-staleness via digest | `src/design_run/runbook.rs` (`Step::material`) | `src/coverage.rs` |
| Failure carrying its cause | `src/regression.rs` (`Unobtainable { why }`) | `src/coverage.rs` (bare `Unobtainable`, 6+ causes fused) |
| Zero-result never reads as green | `src/regression.rs` (INV-5) | `src/coverage.rs` — **repaired**, ISS-324 |
| Callee cannot self-declare success | `runbook.rs` (`Discharge::verified`) | `coverage.rs` (exit code is the claim) |

**Corollary, and it has now held five times for five: check the neighbouring
subsystem before proposing new structure here.** Every repair below is a
transplant, not an invention.

## What this RFC owns, and what stays in RFC-027

Stated explicitly, because two deliberation artifacts over one surface is the
semantic smear both documents argue against.

**RFC-029 owns *proving*** — how a criterion binds to the thing that proves it,
what evidence must carry, and where that contract is governed:

* the criterion ↔ proof link (`IMP-409`);
* binding identity and evidence staleness (`ISS-325`);
* failure cause on observed outcomes (`IMP-408`);
* the SPEC-002 requirement gap that governs all of the above (`CHR-058`);
* the plan reader's discarded authored links (`ISS-321`);
* the specification home for the plan content model (`IMP-382`);
* RFC-027's `H6` (plan criteria reuse the executable coverage seam) and `H13`
  (proof subjects without understanding source languages), with their evidence.

**RFC-027 keeps *discovering*** — undeclared paths, drift, and who may say what
about them: progressive discovery proposals, conformance explanation and
disposition, the execution-capsule authority model, bounded autonomy and
semantic arbitration (`H12`), and the `H9` obligation-graph rejection.

**The joint.** RFC-027's Stage 4 (*proof projection and negative controls*) is
the seam. Its sequencing constraints move here; its status as a stage of
RFC-027's prove-value sequence stays there. Neither document should restate the
other's disposition — cite across the boundary instead.

## Discussion

### 1. The convergence — one unowned fact, found twice

A phase's exit criteria (`EX-N`) are its obligations. Its verification criteria
(`VT` / `VA` / `VH`) are how they are proved. **No authored field connects
them.** The mapping lives only in the author's prose.

The obligation study reached this from the criterion end: of eight candidate
obligation fields tested against RFC-003's derivability rule, this was the only
one not already owned. Identity and order are owned by `REQ-441`, statement by
`EX-N.text`, requirement links by the per-phase `requirements` array, refinement
lineage by `REQ-442`, validation by `REQ-443`, phase actionability by
`compute_next_phases()`.

The proof-binding study reached the same gap from the binding end: a coverage
entry is not bound to the check definition that produced it.

Neither study knew what the other was doing. They are two views of one missing
identity — *this proof belongs to that obligation* — and the consequence is that
**no consumer can derive which obligations are proved, which are unproved, or
what an obligation's evidence is.**

It survived the ownership test that the other seven candidates failed for a
specific reason worth preserving: **it is a field on an existing row, not a new
entity.** That is the shape a repair here should take.

### 2. Binding identity — `ISS-325`

`CoverageEntry` persists `check: Option<VtCheck>` **verbatim and undigested**. An
entry therefore goes stale only when its *evidence's* Git anchor ages. Change the
binding — swap the matcher pattern, change the command, retarget the alias — and
the prior `Verified` stands unchallenged.

So *"the criterion did not change"* and *"the proof did not change"* are
indistinguishable to the engine, and **only the second is currently checked.**

The mechanism exists one subsystem over. `Step::material()`
(`src/design_run/runbook.rs`) builds a netstring-framed, version-tagged digest
over a step's whole definition, so any edit makes the discharge stale by
construction. `DEC-101` states the principle this RFC should copy:

> *an id solves reference, not equivalence.*

Positive control for the absence: `digest` appears **34×** in `runbook.rs` and
**0×** in `coverage.rs` (verified 2026-08-08).

### 3. Failure cause — `IMP-408`

`RunOutcome::Unobtainable` fuses at least six distinct conditions — resolve
error, spawn failure, wall-clock timeout, empty or unreadable glob match set,
unparseable hand-edited regex, empty argv — into `CoverageStatus::Blocked`, and
`CoverageEntry` persists no reason, exit code, or captured output.

A `Blocked` cell therefore **cannot be triaged without re-running it**. The
information existed at the moment of failure and was discarded.

`FailureSet::Unobtainable { why: String }` (`src/regression.rs`) already carries
its cause. Same transplant, different neighbour.

### 4. The governing gap — `CHR-058`, and why it gates

`REQ-254`–`REQ-257` are the four SPEC-002 requirements governing the executable
verification seam: runnable check identity, real command execution, the
production write/withdraw path, and project-agnosticism. **All four are
title-only** — no statement, no acceptance criteria, in either `spec req show` or
the woven `spec show SPEC-002`.

Positive control: `REQ-114` in the same spec carries a full statement and three
acceptance criteria (verified 2026-08-08). This is an authoring gap, not a
spec-wide convention.

RFC-027's `H6` and `H13` both say *"reuse the SPEC-002 seam"*. That presently
means reusing code whose governing requirements assert nothing.

**This is why the order is forced, not chosen.** `ISS-325` and `IMP-408` are both
schema changes to a persisted type in that seam. A schema change cannot be
conformance-checked against requirements that make no claims. The dependency is
authored and live — `doctrine inspect ISS-325` and `IMP-408` both report
`actionable: false` on their inbound `needs → CHR-058` edge, which is `ADR-017`'s
gating mechanism working as designed.

### 5. The discarded links — `ISS-321`

`plan.toml` carries a top-level `[specs]` / `[requirements]` block and per-phase
`specs` / `requirements` arrays. Authors populate them. **The reader drops them.**
`Plan` deserializes only `phases`, and `PlanPhase` carries no such fields, so
every authored link is parsed past and discarded — no gate, projection, or read
surface consumes it.

This item is two things wearing one id, and they should be separated:

1. **Cheap, now, no spec required.** The comment at `src/plan.rs:14-18` asserts
   the link tables *"exist in the file but are empty (no registry yet)"*. The
   second clause is true; **the first is false** — ten plan files populate them.
   A research agent surveying the plan model read that comment, reported the
   tables as scaffold-only corpus-wide, and the false claim survived into a
   written brief. It was caught only on corpus verification. *A stale comment on
   a public struct is load-bearing for agent research, not just for humans* —
   which is itself a finding worth keeping.
2. **Needs a home.** Modelling the tables and giving them a consumer.

authored meanwhile adds more.

### 7. Two tracks, and why the second should start first

The five repairs form two chains that do not block each other.

```text
Track A — coverage seam
  CHR-058 (fill REQ-254..257)
    ├→ ISS-325 (binding digest)
    └→ IMP-408 (Blocked carries cause)

Track B — plan surface
  IMP-382 /spec-tech ("Phase plan surface")   ← DONE: SPEC-031, active
    ├→ IMP-409 (VT→EX link)                     unblocked; D6 settles the shape
    └→ ISS-321 (model the link tables)          unblocked; REQ-467 is the target

Independent of both:
  ISS-321 part 1 — correct the src/plan.rs comment   ← DONE
```

Track A is well-understood ground: `ISS-324` has just been through this seam and
its residual finding is recorded below. Track B is larger, but it is the artefact
**four separate items are waiting on**, and it is where delay compounds.

> **Amended 2026-08-08.** Track B's head is done — `SPEC-031` is active, so both
> its dependents now have a governed host to descend from and neither is
> blocked on specification any more. The advice to start Track B first is spent;
> **Track A is now the unstarted chain**, still gated on `CHR-058` exactly as
> drawn. Note what did *not* change: the two Track B items still need slices and
> implementation — a settled specification unblocks them, it does not do them.

### 8. What `ISS-324` left behind

`ISS-324` (resolved 2026-08-08) removed the literal-command matcher exemption
that let `exit 0` on zero selected tests read as `Verified`. The corpus query
that authorised the clean break also surfaced a worse sibling: the
`[exit-code-only]` report flag tested `matcher.is_none()` where validation tested
absent-or-blank, so a literal command with `matcher = { pattern = "" }` matched
everything, landed `Verified`, and went unflagged. Both seams now read one
`matcher_is_empty`.

**One fact from it constrains everything above.** The *write* seam is the only
validating seam — `coverage_verify::run` never re-validates entries read from
disk, so a hand-authored `coverage.toml` reaches the runner ungoverned. That is
why the exit-code-only apparatus was kept rather than deleted as dead, and it
means any repair here that relies on write-time validation has an unguarded back
door.

**Note the shape.** `ISS-324` was *not* the has-it/lacks-it pattern. It was **one
rule written twice inside a single subsystem, drifting apart.** That is a second
way this corpus grows false greens, and reviewers of the repairs below should
watch for both.

## Open questions

1. ~~Does the `VT`→`EX` link belong on the `VT` row, on the `EX` row, or as a
   separate mapping? (Specification question; `IMP-382`'s `/spec-tech` half.)~~
   **Settled 2026-08-08 — on the `VT` row, inverse derived** (`SPEC-031` D6,
   `REQ-468`). Unbuilt: `Plan`/`PlanPhase` carry no `deny_unknown_fields`, so
   authoring the field today parses clean and is silently dropped.
2. What exactly must the binding digest cover — command, matcher, adapter
   identity, resolved subject set? `Step::material()` digests *the whole step
   definition*; the analogous boundary for a check is not obvious.
3. Should `Blocked`'s cause be a free-text `why` (as `regression.rs` has it) or a
   closed enum? Free text is transplantable today; an enum is queryable. The six
   fused conditions are known, which argues the closed set is reachable.
4. Given that the write seam is the only validating seam (§8), does any repair
   here need a read-time validation pass, or is that a separate defect?
5. Does the plan reader modelling `[specs]`/`[requirements]` need a named
   consumer before it lands, per RFC-003 derivability and `IMP-382`'s own
   boundary? `REQ-439` AC-2 may already be that consumer. **Narrowed
   2026-08-08:** `SPEC-031` `REQ-467` now requires the reader to model both
   surfaces and a read surface to expose them, so the *requirement* exists. The
   derivability question is not thereby answered — a requirement to expose is
   not yet a consumer that reads — but it is now a governed obligation rather
   than an open modelling choice.
6. `RFC-027`'s Stage 4 requires cross-ecosystem evidence (pytest, Go, Jest) that
   was never gathered, and its zero-test trace was reasoned from `derive_status`
   rather than executed. Do those preconditions bind this RFC's repairs, or only
   the language-neutrality claim they were raised against?

## Outcome

**Open.** No repair here is authorised for implementation by this document; each
needs its own slice, and the two schema changes need the SPEC-002 amendment
`CHR-058` produces to exist first.

What this RFC does settle is **scope and order**: the cluster is five items in
two chains, the order within each chain is forced by conformance-checkability
rather than preference, and the single artefact gating the larger chain is a
specification half that has not been written.

> **Amended 2026-08-08.** That last clause is spent: the specification half was
> written and activated as `SPEC-031`. Track B's two items are unblocked and
> still unimplemented; **Track A is now the only chain gated on an unwritten
> artefact**, and its gate is `CHR-058` as drawn in § 7. The scope-and-order
> finding itself stands unchanged.
>
> One thing the spec added that this RFC did not anticipate: authoring it
> surfaced that shipped `install/` assets have no conformance signal against the
> specs that anchor them — `[[source]]` anchors are inert, and the obvious
> shortcut (citing the governing id inside the asset) is barred by `POL-002`,
> since install assets ship into projects with no doctrine corpus to resolve
> them against. Filed as `IMP-411`, related to this RFC.

The prior art is unusually clear for a programme this early, because two
independent studies already ran the ownership test and rejected two proposed
ontologies. **A proposal that adds a record, an entity, or a graph here should
expect to fail the same test** — and should check the neighbouring subsystem
first, which has now been the answer five times running.
