# RFC-034: Steward Human Understanding and Agency

**Status: open.** This RFC is a discussion, not a governing decision. Its
supporting evidence and surface map are in `research.md`; claims there should be
rechecked before they become requirements or implementation plans.

## Context

Doctrine promises that people can understand what agents built and why. Its
current answer is chiefly durable intent, decisions, evidence, and ways to read
them later. That is valuable, but the existence of an explanation does not
establish that the person asked to approve a decision can evaluate it, challenge
its framing, or intervene after a period of delegation.

The originating concern is **authority without expertise**. An agent can know
the code, governing documents, and available options better than the person
whose authority it is exercising. It can frame the question, recommend an
answer, critique that answer, and present a convincing summary for acceptance.
Each individual delegation may be sensible while the accumulated pattern leaves
the person less able to recognise when the framing or evidence is wrong. The
external sources in `research.md` add a related concern about the erosion of
oversight capacity; that broader causal claim is a hypothesis here, not an
established fact about Doctrine's users.

The user's reported failure is concrete: consequential documents often arrive
as identifier-heavy summaries or patch lists, and accepting them is easier than
reading the governing text and understanding what changed. RFC-026 also records
human-incomprehensible coordinate language and design documents whose active
meaning is buried in review history. Better access to documents helps, but it
does not by itself decide what the human should understand or provide a useful
occasion to form an independent view.

Doctrine already has relevant pieces: `walkthrough` supports guided reading;
`pair` supports active participation; `elicit` asks the human before revealing
the model's answer; the managed design run distinguishes continuing guidance
from discrete obligations; and the review ledger plus reconciliation process
retain findings and changes to governing truth. The research found no explicit
understanding obligation in the design, audit, reconciliation, or close skills,
and no stored distinction between a person's assent and demonstrated
understanding. RFC-034 asks what, if anything, should be added to those seams.

## Discussion

### Working aim from the author (2026-09-27)

At a sensible default setting for this project, the human author should be able
to describe a feature at **whiteboard level**: identify the systems and
subsystems involved, explain how they communicate, identify consequential
decisions and risks, and defend the important choices. This is one capability
target for an interaction, not a universal requirement or a claim that every
approval proves it was met. A project may expect a deeper grasp throughout, or
vary the expectation sharply between parts of its codebase. The target should
be calibratable for a project and overridable verbally in a session.

The preferred route to that capability is **useful participation while the work
is being shaped**. Invite the human's judgment where their priorities, lived
context, creativity, or ability to challenge the framing can improve an actual
outcome. Their elaboration in that exchange can also build understanding. Do
not make the human write a comprehensive account of a slice to prove they have
one; Doctrine deliberately has agents write most documents with human steering.

### Shared calibration model (developed in discussion, 2026-09-27)

Agentic engineers continually choose how far above the implementation to
operate. The useful point changes with their confidence in the agent harness
and codebase, the importance of the subsystem, urgency, desired velocity, and
their own sustained understanding. They may notice from introspection that
they need more contact with the work. Disappointing slice outcomes or unpleasant
architectural surprises may suggest they delegated too much, while repeated
reliable outcomes or costly interruptions may suggest room to delegate more.
None of these signals diagnoses the cause on its own.

The human and agent should establish a working **altitude** together, observe
what happens, discuss signals of drift, and adjust. The aim is a practical
balance between acceptable risk and velocity, and between delegation and
understanding, as judged by the human with evidence the agent helps make
legible. That includes candid evidence about the agent and harness: rework,
missed constraints, reliable delivery, and limits of the checks performed. The
agent can point out a surprise, a pattern of rework, its own thin account of a
consequential choice, or friction from excessive explanation. It
should offer a possible interpretation and a useful adjustment without claiming
to know the human's mental state. The human can supply context the agent lacks,
choose a different balance, or decline the suggestion.

“Altitude” is shorthand for several **separate dimensions**: how much detail
the human wants, where their judgment participates, and how much bounded
discretion the agent has. More explanation need not give the human every
technical decision; less explanation need not transfer decision authority.
Going deeper may mean direct source reading, `walkthrough`, `pair`, a focused
question, or mock criticism. Going lighter may mean shorter reports, fewer
interruptions, or more improvisation within agreed bounds. Any increase in
decision authority requires the human's direction and remains subject to the
existing gates and accepted governance; the agent must not infer permission
from trust, fatigue, silence, or a successful run.

A project default provides a starting point, not a fixed setting for every
feature. The working posture can vary by session, subsystem, or consequential
decision and can change as feedback arrives. The agent's role is to help the
human recognise and interpret signals, make the available adjustments clear,
and revisit the choice when outcomes warrant it. The practices below are a
repertoire for doing that, not a required sequence or a universal script.

**The human retains authority.** The working posture guides how the agent
explains and offers help; it does not set an acceptance condition.
The agent may suspect that its account has not landed and invite more context,
detail, exposition, `walkthrough`, or `pair`. It must not require the human to
demonstrate understanding before accepting. Existing validity checks remain
separate from this conversational aim.

The author prefers to start with agent instructions, skills, and templates.
Those changes are cheaper to revise than Rust mechanisms and can test which
interactions actually help before structural work is chosen. For the **initial
trial**, the author accepts `.doctrine/governance.md` as the home for a project
default, with a verbal session override. `doctrine boot` reads that file into
the resident `## Governance (project)` section; the former `boot-footer.md`
injection was retired by SL-187 (research §7.1). A default added to that file
reaches new sessions through boot; a verbal override can take effect in the
current conversation. The trial therefore needs no new Rust configuration just
to express the setting.

This does not make every project-specific behaviour overridable. Skills have no
project override path, and the managed design prompts have no project extension
interface yet (IMP-375). ADR-009's existing `[conduct]` setting declares actor
and autonomy per lifecycle state, while this target concerns understanding. If
the trial shows a need for a typed setting or finer stage-specific control,
those are later design questions. A setting must not silently change who may
make a decision.

### Initial calibration (chosen in discussion, 2026-09-27)

Start with a **plain-language project default** in `.doctrine/governance.md`.
For this project's trial, it describes the whiteboard-level understanding the
interaction should normally support, rather than selecting from named levels.
A person can change the depth or form of an explanation verbally during a
session (for example, “go deeper on the data flow” or “give me the short
orientation”). The agent should follow that request immediately and carry it
through the relevant work until the person changes it. A request to change
delegated discretion is a separate instruction about authority, to be handled
within the existing conduct and acceptance rules.

Candidate governance wording for the pilot, to refine before use:

> Work with the human to calibrate explanation, participation, and delegated
> discretion to the project and the work at hand. For this project, normally
> support a whiteboard-level account of consequential features: participating
> systems and their communication, important decisions and reasons, and risks.
> Respect verbal requests for more or less detail. Where the human's judgment
> could improve an outcome, invite it and use the answer. If outcomes, surprises,
> or the interaction suggest a different level of engagement might help, name
> the observed signal and offer a concrete way to go deeper or lighter; do not
> infer a mental state or repeat an offer after a decline. Make consequential
> cited Doctrine documents easy to open with exact read commands and enough
> context to understand why they matter. Only the human can expand the agent's
> decision authority, within existing gates. Never make a demonstration of
> understanding a condition of acceptance.

This wording is a project default, not the whole pilot. Audit and reconciliation
instructions still need to specify how an agent can make relevant material and
calibration choices visible without forcing a question at every gate. Avoid
fixed depth labels until the trial shows that people need them and can use them
consistently.

### Candidate techniques

These are ways to adjust engagement when they serve a real need, not a required
sequence or a quota of prompts:

1. **Offer a guided reading during audit.** Give the human a short route through
   the feature's system map, one consequential behaviour in the code, the tests
   that support it, and a remaining risk. Offer `walkthrough` or `pair` so the
   human can inspect the implementation at their chosen depth. The review
   ledger still owns findings and dispositions; this is a way to read the work.
2. **Offer a guided reading during reconciliation.** At the point where the
   human is asked to accept a consequential change to governing text, offer a
   `walkthrough` of the current passage, proposed passage, and effect on future
   work. Establish how this composes with the existing show-old/show-new
   confirmation step, rather than adding a second generic acceptance prompt.
3. **Make sources easy to open.** When an agent refers to a Doctrine entity in
   a decision, include the exact CLI command for reading it, such as `doctrine
   adr show ADR-023`, alongside a readable name and the relevant passage or
   effect. A command gives the human a route to independent inspection; it does
   not replace enough context to understand the question in front of them.
4. **Notice possible loss of the shared model.** Encourage the agent to notice
   interaction patterns and offer better material or a different mode. Six terse
   `y` replies in succession could justify asking whether a clearer account or
   a code walkthrough would be useful. The agent cannot tell from that pattern
   whether the person is comfortable, outside their expertise, fatigued,
   disengaged, or struggling with inscrutable agent prose. It should not
   diagnose the person, demand an explanation, repeat the offer after a decline,
   or hold acceptance. It should also consider improving its own presentation.
5. **Invite consequential human judgment.** During audit or reconciliation,
   ask a bounded question whose answer can change the risk account, deployment
   advice, or governing text. For example: “What risk remains most concerning
   to you when you consider deployment?” Show enough context to make the
   question answerable, and carry the answer into the work. Do not ask merely
   to make the human perform understanding for the agent.
6. **Offer a short mock challenge.** To help a human prepare to defend a design,
   an agent could offer to play a critical peer. Start from a consequential
   decision, a real review finding and its disposition, or an audit concern;
   then pose one plausible objection and let the human respond, ask for context,
   or reconsider the design. The exchange may expose a weak rationale or a
   missing risk as well as help the human rehearse. Keep the source material
   visible and distinguish a historical objection from a newly simulated one.

   The mock challenge has two possible purposes that should be named before it
   starts: **rehearsal** of the human's account, or **further criticism** of the
   design. A response that needs correction calls for explanation, not a score
   or an acceptance gate. Difficulty defending a choice does not itself prove
   the design is flawed. A substantive design defect discovered in the exchange
   belongs in the actual review or audit process, with a real finding and
   disposition; roleplay is not a parallel ledger. Offer this as an optional,
   bounded interaction, with room for the human to stop or change depth. The
   current RV read surface is still being improved under RFC-032, so a pilot
   may need the agent to bring the relevant finding, response, and evidence
   into view rather than assume the human can retrieve a complete challenge
   history easily.

7. **Offer a lighter posture.** When the human wants more velocity and the
   observed work supports it, propose a bounded area where the agent can make
   routine choices, improvise within stated constraints, and report outcomes or
   exceptions rather than interrupt for each choice. State the bounds and
   return points so the human can decide whether to grant that discretion.

The first evaluation should observe whether the human and agent identify a
useful operating posture, recognise signals that it should change, and make an
adjustment that improves the work or reduces unnecessary friction. It should
also observe whether engagement changes the framing, risks, decisions, or
governing text for the better, and whether it helps the person form a working
model without adding fatigue or scripted ceremony. If a direct signal of
understanding is useful, sample a narrow point outside the acceptance act
rather than requesting an inventory or retrospective essay. A prompt such as
“Two key risks were X and Y; do you remember another?” tests some recall, but
may feel like a quiz and misses judgment. “Which deployment risk concerns you
most?” invites a useful contribution, but is a weaker signal that the wider
slice is understood. The pilot may use
both selectively, with willing participants, while recording what each can and
cannot show. It should also record when the agent supplied the entire framing
and the human had no independent foothold. Prompt-only changes that cannot
produce this evidence would leave the hypothesis untested.

### First pilot boundary (chosen in discussion, 2026-09-27)

The author chose **audit and reconciliation together** for the first pilot.
Audit supplies contact with the implemented systems, code, tests, and risks;
reconciliation supplies contact with the governing text that future work will
inherit. Start with a suitable slice whose audit has consequential material to
read and whose reconciliation has a real decision or text change. A no-op
reconciliation can still inform the audit half, but cannot test the proposed
reconciliation interaction.

The author also agreed to include **optional mock criticism** in this pilot
when a consequential review or audit finding gives it a useful basis. The
agent may offer a short peer-style challenge, but no challenge is required per
stage and declining one changes no acceptance condition.

These late stages can test legibility and invite changes to risk treatment or
governing text. They cannot, by themselves, test the fuller aim of human input
shaping a feature from its first questions. Use the pilot's findings to decide
how to carry the practice upstream, rather than treating a successful audit
conversation as evidence that the whole lifecycle is covered.

Use `.doctrine/governance.md` for the pilot's project default and accept a
verbal override in the session. Trial changes to the relevant skill prose may
be needed to make the invitations and reading routes reliable; their exact text
and ownership remain to be designed. Neither stage gains a comprehension gate,
an extra acceptance act, or a requirement that the human explain themselves.
The pilot should record where the human requested or declined more context,
which source material was actually useful, where their input changed the work,
whether either party proposed an adjustment to the working posture, and what
felt repetitive or opaque. One slice is a usability probe, not proof of lasting
skill, independence, or well-calibrated delegation across a whole project.

### Questions to settle

1. **How is the working altitude chosen and revised?** Which observations
   warrant proposing a change, and how should the agent present them without
   diagnosing the human or mistaking its own poor explanation for the human's
   lack of understanding? When should the current posture be stated explicitly
   rather than inferred from conversation? How can a project default remain
   useful when one subsystem needs much closer attention and another permits
   broad delegation?
2. **What may be delegated?** Understanding can be selective. Which choices
   merit an offer of direct engagement, which can be delegated honestly, and
   how does the human choose that boundary? How should the agent offer bounded
   discretion without conflating it with explanatory depth or participation?
   A person can want a deep explanation without owning every technical choice,
   or delegate detail without delegating product intent.
3. **What follows the first pilot?** Product inquiry and technical design pose
   different questions from audit and reconciliation. What evidence from the
   first pilot would justify extending the posture upstream? What should happen
   when a technical discovery reopens a product assumption?
4. **When might the human speak first?** An invited prediction or independent
   judgment can expose anchoring, improve a decision, and build a working model.
   Which audit or reconciliation questions could change the work through the
   human's answer? What factual context must be shown before asking? Used
   indiscriminately, a question becomes a quiz or an approval ritual; it is
   never a condition of acceptance.
5. **What does direct inspection look like?** At audit, a short route through
   one promise, its implementation, its tests, and a failure case might reveal
   more than a list of findings. At reconciliation, the person may need to read
   the relevant passage in place and distinguish a corrected description from
   a changed requirement. How much of either can fit within a real decision?
6. **How would this be learned and measured?** The research found no observation
   base for this theme. Assent, document views, and self-reported confidence are
   weak evidence of understanding. Which narrow samples are worth the
   interruption, and how can the trial distinguish improved outcomes from a
   stronger signal of comprehension? What would show that a change in operating
   posture reduced risk or friction, rather than merely changing the amount of
   agent prose? What small trial could reveal benefit, fatigue, and cases where
   an agent's framing still dominates?
7. **Where should a settled practice live?** Candidate seams include project
   instructions or conduct settings, lifecycle skills, managed design-run
   prompts, and CLI reading surfaces. The first project default can live in
   `.doctrine/governance.md` as plain language with a verbal session override.
   What evidence would justify fixed levels or typed configuration later? How
   should an override survive a long session or handover? An RFC outcome should
   identify the owning surface for each obligation and avoid duplicating
   adjacent work in RFC-026, RFC-030, RFC-031, RFC-032, and RFC-033.
8. **How should mock criticism work?** When the human wants to rehearse a
   defense, how should the agent select one consequential challenge from design
   review history or audit evidence? How should it distinguish preparation for
   peer scrutiny from a fresh design review, and route a genuine new finding
   back to the ledger? What would make the exchange useful rather than
   performative or needlessly adversarial?

These are discussion questions, not a proposed requirement set. An interaction
setting cannot certify a mental state; an acknowledgement must not be treated as
evidence of comprehension. Equally, an agent's suspicion of misunderstanding
must not become an authority claim over the human's decision.

## Outcome

Open. The author has ruled out an agentic understanding gate: the agent may
offer help, and the human decides whether to take it. The author accepts
`.doctrine/governance.md` for a first project-default trial, with verbal session
override and no fixed levels, and chose audit plus reconciliation for the first
pilot. The preferred mechanism is meaningful human participation during the
work; narrow samples may supplement it, without a comprehensive recap or
acceptance gate. The broader aim is a shared, revisable calibration of
explanation, participation, and bounded delegation, responsive to outcomes and
the human's own assessment. The whiteboard target is this project's proposed
default, not a universal level. A short, optional mock challenge belongs in the
first pilot when a consequential finding gives it a useful basis. Candidate
governance wording is recorded above. The detailed trial and later sequence
remain unresolved.
