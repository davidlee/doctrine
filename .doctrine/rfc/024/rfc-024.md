# RFC-024: Product altitude taxonomy and the Scenario entity

> Status: **open** — two modelling amendments recorded together, with their
> source briefs preserved under `briefs/`. Asserts no canon. Adopting either
> half needs an ADR (the taxonomy shape) and a Revision (the PRD-002 / PRD-012
> text it moves), not this document.

## Context

A product spec already carries an altitude. `ProductLevel` (`src/spec.rs:703`)
is a closed four-rung ladder — `domain | capability | feature | story` — minted
as a deliberate mirror of `C4Level` (`context | container | component | code`),
and the doc comment at `src/spec.rs:698` states the intended correspondence
outright: *"feature≈component, story≈code"*. Since IMP-069 the ladder is load-
bearing rather than advisory: `parent_rank_findings` (`src/registry.rs:297`)
refuses a rank inversion (a child at a coarser level than its parent) and a rank
gap (a child skipping more than one level), for product and tech specs alike.

The mirror is the problem. `Story` was placed at rank 3 because `Code` sits at
C4 rank 3, not because a user story is a smaller structural part of a feature.
Two briefs, authored separately and now attached here, arrive at that from
opposite directions:

- **`briefs/product-taxonomy.md`** — a story is not a finer-grained *part* of a
  product; it is a *path through* one. It may traverse several features or
  several capabilities, and its scope varies wildly. Placing it beneath `Feature`
  in a containment hierarchy misrepresents most of them. Replacing it with `Epic`
  trades one error for another: an epic is transient delivery coordination, not
  evergreen product truth.
- **`briefs/scenarios.md`** — the useful concept underneath a user story is
  broader than the story sentence. A **scenario** is a concrete path through the
  product or system under stated conditions, and it is orthogonal to *both*
  ladders rather than a rung on either.

The corpus makes the first amendment nearly free. `product_level` is set on
exactly three specs today (PRD-016, PRD-017, PRD-018), all at `capability`.
**Nothing in the corpus is a `story`.** The cost of retiring the variant is the
enum arm, the template comment (`install/templates/spec-product.toml:11`), the
mirror doc comment, and two test fixtures (`src/spec.rs:3184`, `:4339`).

## Discussion

### Half one — three rungs, and recursion at the floor

The taxonomy brief's position is a three-level product ladder with the floor
recursive:

```text
Domain → Capability → Feature → Feature → …
```

Both parent and child of a recursive pair remain `Feature`; recursion adds depth
without adding altitude. Product and technical classification therefore stop
being depth-matched — three product rungs against four C4 rungs — and are held to
be *approximately comparable forms of zoom* with no one-to-one equivalence
claimed. That asymmetry is the point, not a defect to be papered over.

**The validator already admits this.** `parent_rank_findings` computes
`delta = child_rank - parent_rank` and flags only `delta < 0` (inversion) and
`delta > 1` (gap). `delta == 0` passes — `parent_rank_same_level_is_clean`
(`src/registry.rs:764`) asserts exactly that, for a same-level pair. So
feature-parents-feature needs no new rule. What it needs is to stop being an
accident of a permissive check and become a stated property of the model, with
the invariants the brief lists (acyclic; each child coherent behaviour in its own
right; a child narrows or composes its parent; a parent may retain behaviour its
children do not cover; components, tasks, and migrations are *not* child
features).

Three things about the recursive floor are worth arguing before anyone commits:

- **Depth is unbounded by the data model.** The brief accepts arbitrary depth in
  storage and pushes the discouragement into presentation and authoring tooling.
  That is defensible but it is a promise the tooling does not currently keep, and
  it interacts with `spec show` / `explore` rendering.
- **Same-level parenting is currently legal for tech specs too.** If the product
  floor becomes deliberately recursive, the same permission on the C4 side —
  component-parents-component — is either a matching intentional affordance or a
  hole. The check does not distinguish them today. Deciding one should mean
  deciding the other.
- **A single canonical parent stays preferable.** Cross-cutting behaviour is
  routed to explicit relations, requirements, or scenarios rather than to a
  second structural parent — which is what makes the hierarchy navigable, and
  what makes half two necessary rather than optional.

### Half two — Scenario as a cross-cutting entity

Retiring `Story` from the ladder leaves the thing it was badly modelling with
nowhere to live. The scenarios brief proposes `Scenario`: who participates, under
what preconditions, driven by what event or goal, producing what outcome —
orthogonal to product altitude and C4 level both.

```text
Product structure:    Domain → Capability → Feature → Feature…
Technical structure:  System Context → Container → Component → Code
Cross-sectional:      Scenario
```

The brief carries one entity with a `perspective` facet (`product | technical |
operational`) rather than two kinds, related to structure by `traverses` /
`involves`, to obligation by `exercises` / `demonstrates`, and refined
product→technical by `refines`. A user story becomes a *notation* — a rendering,
an optional `story` field, or a transient delivery artefact pointing at an
evergreen scenario — not an entity type. The ontology models the scenario, not
the sentence template.

Two claims in the brief are already the implemented shape and should be read as
statements of intent rather than proposals:

- **Requirements are already n:n against specs.** Membership is a per-spec
  `members.toml` row (`requirement`, sticky `label`, advisory `order`), so one
  `REQ-NNN` can be a member of several specs at different altitudes with no
  change. The brief's real content here is normative: **ancestry supplies
  context, not inheritance.** A child feature inherits its product location and
  nothing else; tooling may *surface* an ancestor's requirements as candidates,
  but ancestry alone must not create an unstated obligation. That is a rule about
  what `spec show` and any future rollup are permitted to imply, and it has no
  enforcement point today.
- **`Epic` is out.** Delivery structure (initiative → epic → slice) may reference
  product specs and requirements without entering the product ontology. Doctrine
  already keeps that separation — slices and backlog items are the delivery axis
  — so this is a boundary being restated, not moved.

What is genuinely new is the cost. `Scenario` is a new first-class kind: id
reservation, entity-engine scaffolding, a relation vocabulary registered against
the cross-corpus contract, `show` / `list` / `validate` surface, and a governance
home — either an extension of PRD-002 or a PRD of its own. The brief's own gating
test is the honest one: a scenario earns independent identity when it needs reuse
across specs, relations to several features or components, refinement into both
views, requirement traceability, test/evidence linkage, independent revision
history, or stable reference from reviews. A small example clarifying one
paragraph stays inline. **The two halves are separable in delivery** — retiring
`Story` does not depend on `Scenario` existing — but they are not separable in
justification, which is why they are recorded together.

## Open questions

Carried from the briefs, plus what the grounding raised.

**Taxonomy**

- **Q1 — Does retiring `Story` need a migration path at all?** Zero specs use it.
  A hard removal is a parse error for any out-of-tree corpus that adopted it;
  accept-and-warn is cheap insurance. Which, and for how long?
- **Q2 — Does the recursive floor extend to the C4 ladder?** Same-level parenting
  passes the check for tech specs today. Intentional affordance or hole?
- **Q3 — Where is nesting depth discouraged?** The brief puts it in presentation
  and authoring, not the model. What does that mean concretely for `spec show`,
  `explore`, and the map renderer?

**Scenario**

- **Q4 — Informative by default, or explicitly normative?** (Brief OQ-1.) If a
  scenario's outcomes can ever bind, the boundary against requirements needs a
  sharper statement than "unless the model declares otherwise".
- **Q5 — One entity with a `perspective` facet, or separate product and technical
  kinds?** (Brief OQ-2.) The facet reading is what makes `refines` work across the
  boundary; the split reading is what keeps the field sets honest.
- **Q6 — Promotion of inline scenarios: automatic or by explicit author action?**
  (Brief OQ-3.)
- **Q7 — Variants as fields, or as separate scenarios linked by `refines` /
  `alternative_to` / `fails_during`?** (Brief OQ-4.)
- **Q8 — Do test and evidence relations land in v1 or a later extension?**
  (Brief OQ-5.) This is the question with the largest blast radius — it reaches
  `coverage` and the reconciliation engine (SPEC-002), where verification
  evidence already has an owner.
- **Q9 — Governance home.** Extension of PRD-002, or a PRD of its own?

**Both**

- **Q10 — What enforces "ancestry is context, not inheritance"?** Nothing does
  today because nothing rolls requirements up. The rule is cheap to state now and
  expensive to retrofit once a rollup exists.

## Outcome

Open. Nothing is decided.

The retirement of `Story` is the cheap, well-evidenced half: the corpus is clean,
the validator already permits the recursive floor, and the change is an enum arm
plus documentation. It is worth doing on its own merits, and it is the half that
can move first.

`Scenario` is the expensive half and the reason the first one is safe — without
somewhere for cross-cutting paths to live, retiring `Story` removes a rung
without replacing what it was (badly) covering. Q5 is the question to answer
first: one entity with a perspective facet or two kinds decides the field set,
the relation vocabulary, and whether `refines` crosses a kind boundary. Q8 is the
one to answer second, because it decides whether this touches SPEC-002 or stays
clear of it.
