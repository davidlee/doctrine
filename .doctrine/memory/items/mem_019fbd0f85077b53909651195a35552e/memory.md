A taxonomy question — "is this obligation a discrete step or an ongoing lens?" —
is a property of the artefact's **text and semantics**, not of any run. No
observation of an agent's behaviour can establish it, because behaviour tells you
how one agent acted on one occasion and the claim is a universal negative about
the obligation.

**Why.** SL-233 RV-325 burned rounds 5–10 trying to infer *"this obligation has
no truthful completion moment"* from a single moderated exercise. Every round
produced a subtler inference and every one had the same defect. The last inverted
a soundly-**negative** test (subject absent at discharge ⇒ the step is not
complete) into a positive one (subject present at some turn ⇒ a truthful
completion moment exists) — affirming the consequent, which exhaustiveness does
not rescue.

**How to apply.**
1. When a test keeps failing review on a *new* argument each round, suspect the
   mechanism rather than its specification. Ask: **what property am I trying to
   derive, and where does that property actually live?**
2. If it lives in the text, gate it at authoring: require the artefact to state
   the property itself, in a form that is truthful and checkable, and classify
   anything that cannot state it as the other kind. That is decidable over the
   whole set, before any run, and re-runnable by anyone — a far stronger
   falsifier than one post-hoc observation.
3. Leave the run to collect what runs can collect: **adherence to a stated
   condition**, which is mechanical.
4. Watch the direction of an existing sound test before reusing it. Conservative
   negative tests ("absence proves not-done") do not invert.

**Owner's framing, which is the general form:** we do not build taxonomical truth
from first principles — that is what design is. Evidence feeds design; the owner
arbitrates whether the design is good. Building elaborate machinery to make one
observation settle a design judgement is the category error.
