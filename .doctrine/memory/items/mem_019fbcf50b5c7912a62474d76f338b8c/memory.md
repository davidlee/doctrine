A responder's default reflex on a contested finding is to repair the artefact.
Sometimes the contest is defeated by the text as it already stands — and the
right move is then to **reject the contest and write the entailment that made
the misreading available**, not to change the rule.

**Why.** RV-325 round 8 (SL-233 F-15): the contest claimed a pre-registered
falsifier could not compel the expensive outcome at N=1, because it read a
"one-shot per step" clause as the only compulsion route. Four surfaces already
said otherwise ("failing to produce one compels the expensive one"). But the
*entailment* that gave that route teeth — the cheap arm's artefact test is
unmeetable exactly under the cause that warrants the expensive arm — was
load-bearing and stated nowhere. A careful reader eight rounds in missed it, so
a verifier under the same criterion would too.

**How to apply.**
1. Verify the contest against every tier that carries the rule before answering
   — plan/criteria, sketch, diagram, and the governing knowledge record.
2. If the text defeats it, ask the second question: *was the contest's reading
   available?* An entailment that is load-bearing but unstated reads as absent.
3. Repair the exposition on every surface, marked `clarified / no rule change`,
   so the record shows a rejected contest rather than a silent capitulation.
4. Check the contest's implied remedy against earlier findings — reinstating a
   defect a prior round removed is the repair-reproduces-the-defect pattern
   arriving backwards.

governs the sweep after a repair; this one governs whether to repair at all.
