Six observed instances across doctrine adversarial reviews. The shape is
constant: a finding names an axis ("this asserts too much", "this evidence
supports more than one consequence"), and the repair travels along that axis
past the correct point, landing in a new defect facing the other way.

The clean case (RV-323 F-13, another thread): a finding said the evidence
supported more than one consequence; the fix added a consequence supported by
none.

My own instance, same review: F-14 said an assertion's *scope* was too wide
(I1's object-count clause covered advance-stage refusals it could not survive).
The repair scoped it — correctly — and then claimed the newly-carved-out path
"cannot deterministically be produced by the rig". Stronger than the evidence:
an injected delay produces it. The finding was "you asserted too much"; the
draft repair asserted too much in the other direction. Corrected to *reachable
but unexercised*.

## Why it recurs

A finding supplies a direction, not a destination. Under remediation pressure
the direction reads as "further is safer" — so a scope that was too wide becomes
an impossibility claim, a claim that was too strong becomes an unqualified
retraction, a missing guard becomes a guard nothing can trip.

## The check

Before landing a repair, ask of the repair *itself*, not the finding:

- Does the evidence support this new claim, or only the *removal* of the old
  one? Removing an overclaim does not license its inverse.
- If the repair asserts an impossibility, un-reachability, or "never", is that
  demonstrated — or is it just the far end of the axis?
- State residuals as **recorded asymmetries**, not as closed cases. An
  unexercised path stated as impossible is how a gap stops being looked at.
- A repair that adds machinery beyond the sentence must justify the addition
  mechanically, and say so in the disposition so the raiser can contest it
  rather than inherit it silently.

about sweeping *sideways* for siblings; this one is about not travelling too far
*forward* on the one you have.
